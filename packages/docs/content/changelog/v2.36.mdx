The AIOStreams app now runs on **Android**, on phones, tablets and TVs, Fire TV included,
and on **LG and Samsung TVs**, as well as in a browser and the desktop app. On a TV it has
a layout made for the remote, with navigation and a home page you can style to suit you.

The desktop and Android apps **download** movies and episodes to watch offline, and every
app keeps the pages you browse, so it opens straight away and still works without a
connection. The player has been reworked, with **touch gestures**, **hold to play faster**
and an **episode list**, and **trailers** play in the app. The app also passes over **filler
and recap episodes**, asks **whether you are still watching**, remembers your **audio and
subtitle picks** for each show, can ask **who is watching** every time it opens, sends
versions to **the player of your choice**, your own mpv included, and **suggests titles** as
you type a search.

For everyone, AIOStreams now **reads the files you play**. The first play of a file records
its real audio and subtitle tracks, and every stream list after that shows them, in Stremio
as well as the apps.

For self-hosters, a **Media info** page on the dashboard shows what has been read, and
instances can share what they read with each other. If you run an instance, consider
turning on [**RemuxDB contributions**](#contribute-to-remuxdb): what your instance reads then
fills in stream lists on every other instance too.

## The AIOStreams app, everywhere

The [AIOStreams app](/apps) is AIOStreams' own app for watching. It signs in to your
configuration's [media server](/apps/media-server), which runs on your AIOStreams instance,
so there is nothing else to host. Your catalogs are its libraries, and your streams are its
versions, sorted and named by your formatter as they are in Stremio.

![The AIOStreams app on a TV, a computer and a phone: Sintel playing with styled subtitles on the TV, the home page with Practical Magic featured on the computer, and Severance's show page on the phone](/changelog/v2.36/apps.webp)

| Your device                       | Get it                                                                                                        |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Any device with a browser         | Nothing to install: press **Open in browser** under **Save & Install → AIOStreams app** on the configure page |
| Windows, Mac and Linux            | The [desktop app](/apps/desktop#download)                                                                     |
| Android phones and tablets        | The [Android app](/apps/android#download), new in this release                                                |
| Android TV, Google TV and Fire TV | The [Android app](/apps/android#on-a-tv), new in this release                                                 |
| LG and Samsung TVs                | The [TV apps](/apps/smart-tv), new in this release                                                            |
| iPhone, iPad and Apple TV         | A browser on iPhone and iPad, or a [Jellyfin app](/apps/jellyfin)                                             |

It is the same app everywhere: the same home page, title pages and settings. What it plays
with differs. A browser only plays what it can decode, the desktop app plays nearly anything
with mpv, the Android app has ExoPlayer and mpv, and the TV apps use the TV's own player.
**Save & Install** on the configure page now lists every one of them.

### Android and Android TV

The **Android app** runs on phones, tablets and TVs: Android TV, Google TV, Fire TV and the
NVIDIA Shield. The video plays natively, so MKV files, HEVC and AV1 video, HDR and Dolby
Vision, surround sound and styled subtitles all play.

- **Two engines.** ExoPlayer, Android's own player, uses less battery. mpv decodes nearly
  anything. **Automatic**, the default under **Settings → Playback → Player → Engine**, plays
  with ExoPlayer and switches to mpv for a video ExoPlayer can't play, and when a video fails,
  the error screen offers the other engine.
- **Styled subtitles**, such as most anime releases, look the same in both engines, drawn by
  the renderer mpv uses.
- **Dolby Vision** plays as Dolby Vision where the screen and the device's decoder take it.
  Profile 7 releases are converted to profile 8.1 on the fly where the decoder only takes
  profile 8, and a screen without Dolby Vision gets the HDR10 or HLG picture underneath.
- **On a phone or tablet**, swipe to seek and to change brightness and volume, pinch to fill
  the screen, and keep watching in **picture-in-picture** or with the screen off. Movies,
  episodes and whole seasons [download](#downloads-and-saved-pages) to watch offline.
- **On a TV**, the app opens in the [layout made for the remote](#on-a-tv). It can switch the
  TV to each video's frame rate and play with tunneled playback. Surround audio passes through
  to the TV or receiver as it is whenever they say they decode it, which keeps Atmos. On
  Android TV and Google TV, the search page has a microphone for voice search.
- **Other players.** Versions can open in VLC, MX Player, mpv or another video app instead.
- **Updates.** The app checks for updates and installs them under **Settings → Android app**,
  from releases or nightly builds.

It needs Android 7.0 or later and Android System WebView or Chrome 109 or later. Download it
from the [Android release](https://github.com/Viren070/AIOStreams/releases/tag/android):
arm64-v8a for most phones, tablets and the Shield, armeabi-v7a for Fire TV sticks and most
other TV boxes. On a TV, the **Downloader** app fetches it with a code, **5368982** for
armeabi-v7a and **2899297** for arm64-v8a. The [Android app guide](/apps/android) has the
rest.

### LG and Samsung TVs

The app also comes packaged for **LG TVs** (webOS) and **Samsung TVs** (Tizen), with the same
layout for the remote. They play through the TV's own player, so the TV decides which formats
play. Neither TV's store carries them, so they install from a file, the way developers
install their own apps:

- **LG.** With Developer Mode on, install `aiostreams-webos.ipk` with webOS Dev Manager, or add
  the app's repository to the Homebrew Channel, which installs it and keeps it up to date.
- **Samsung.** Apps2Samsung, or Samsung's Tizen Studio, signs `aiostreams-tizen.wgt` for your
  TV and installs it.

They are made for TVs from 2023 or later (webOS 23 and Tizen 7). On an older TV, the app says
it may not work and offers to try anyway. Both packages are on the
[TV apps release](https://github.com/Viren070/AIOStreams/releases/tag/smart-tv), and the
[smart TV guide](/apps/smart-tv) walks through installing them.

<Callout type="warn">
  The TV apps have only been tried in LG's and Samsung's TV emulators so far,
  not on real TVs. Expect rough edges, and please report how they run on yours.
</Callout>

### On a TV

On Android TV, Fire TV and the LG and Samsung TVs, the app opens in a layout made for the
remote. The arrows move between everything on screen and come back to where you were in each
row, **Back** goes back, and holding **Select** on a title opens its menu.

- **Your navigation.** Under **Settings → Interface**, the navigation runs along the top as
  floating pills, a bar or a fade, or down the side as a docked rail, a floating rail or a
  button in the corner. It sits on a solid background, frosted glass or liquid glass, on
  phones and computers too.
- **The featured title** at the top of home rotates, and **Left** and **Right** step through
  its titles. It can instead follow the card you are on, or follow it from the corner.
- **Signing in** suits the remote. Approve the code **Quick Connect** shows on the configure
  page, and pick who is watching from a picker made for the arrows.
- **In the player**, **Select** on the seek bar plays or pauses, **Select** presses a skip
  button as soon as it shows, and the next episode's card comes up with **Play** ready to
  press.

## New in the app

Everything here works in every app, in a browser too, unless it says otherwise.

### Downloads and saved pages

The desktop app, and the Android app on phones and tablets, **download** movies, episodes and
whole seasons. Press the download button on a title or beside an episode and pick the version,
as you would to play it. For a show, download the season on screen, its unwatched episodes or
the whole show, and the app finds a version for each episode, keeping to the release you
picked where it can.

- **The Downloads page** shows what is downloading, with pause and resume. A download that
  needs you, because no version matched or it failed, waits there with **Pick a version** and
  **Try again**.
- **Finished downloads play without a connection.** Where you stopped and what you watched
  reach the server once the app can reach it again, dated when they happened, so a later play
  on another device still wins.
- **Settings → Downloads** sets the folder in the desktop app, how many download at once, and
  whether subtitles are saved beside the video. The Android app can wait for Wi-Fi.

Every app, in a browser too, also **keeps the pages and artwork you browse** on the device.
They open straight away from there and refresh from the server behind them, and without a
connection, everything kept can still be browsed. **Settings → Cache** sets how much space it
uses, 500 MB unless you change it, and what it keeps.

### The player

The player's controls have been reworked, and they are bigger on TVs and large screens.

- **Touch.** Tap to show or hide the controls, and double tap a side to skip back or forward.
  Swipe across the video to seek, or up and down on the left for brightness and on the right
  for volume. Pinch out to fill the screen, or in to fit the picture, and lock the screen
  against stray touches.
- **Hold to play faster.** Press and hold the video, hold Space, or hold **Select** on a TV
  remote, and it plays at the **Held speed** under **Settings → Playback** until you let go.
- **Episodes.** The **Episodes** button lists the show's seasons and episodes beside the
  video, and picking one plays it, in the same release where there is one.
- **Versions** open in a sheet beside the video, so it stays in view while you pick another.
- **Dolby Vision or HDR10.** In the desktop and Android apps, turn off **Dolby Vision** under
  **Settings → Playback → Video** to play the HDR10 picture most Dolby Vision releases also
  carry, for a screen where Dolby Vision looks dim or flickers.
- Skip a few times in a row, and the seek bar shows where you will land straight away.
- In the Android app, the player opens full screen, in landscape on a phone.

![Sintel paused in the desktop app's player, with a gold sign reading The land of the gatekeepers above a styled subtitle](/changelog/v2.36/player-desktop.webp)

### Trailers

**Trailer** on a title's page plays its trailer in the app. When the metadata lists several,
such as a teaser and the main trailer, **Trailers** lists them by name with a picture of
each, and the player steps between them with previous and next.

The desktop and Android apps play YouTube trailers in their own player, and a browser plays
them in YouTube's. The LG and Samsung TV apps can't play YouTube trailers yet, and offer to
open the trailer's page instead.

### Fillers and recaps

![Naruto's fourth season in the app, where episode 4 is followed by episodes 5 to 8, each marked Filler](/changelog/v2.36/fillers.webp)

Long-running anime are full of filler episodes, which don't follow the original story, and
recaps. When your metadata addon marks them, the app shows a **Filler** or **Recap** mark on
those episodes, and playing on to the next episode passes over them, as do the player's next
and previous buttons. You can still play one yourself.

Both are on by default. Turn them off with **Skip filler episodes** and **Skip recap episodes**
under **Settings → Playback → Next episode**. The marks come from the metadata addon:
AIOMetadata marks anime episodes when it uses MyAnimeList for anime, and any addon can mark
its own (see [For addon developers](#for-addon-developers)).

### Are you still watching?

With **Play it automatically** on, the app no longer plays on for hours to an empty room. Once
three episodes have played in a row without a key, click, remote or gamepad press, it pauses
and asks before starting the next. Change the count, or turn it off, with **Ask if you are
still watching** under **Settings → Playback → Next episode**.

Playing on also starts sooner: the next episode's versions are looked up two minutes before
the prompt, so they are ready when the countdown ends.

### Audio and subtitle tracks

- **Languages in order.** List your audio and subtitle languages in order of preference under
  **Settings → Audio** and **Settings → Subtitles**. **Original language** is one of the
  choices, and plays each title in the language it was made in, Japanese for anime or Korean
  for a K-drama. The server picks each version's tracks from the same lists, so Jellyfin apps
  start in your languages too.
- **Subtitle types in order.** When a version has more than one subtitle in your language,
  **Subtitle types** under **Settings → Subtitles** decides which shows: full subtitles, SDH
  with sounds described, forced subtitles for foreign parts only, or signs and songs. Full
  subtitles come first unless you change it, and commentary always comes last. The server
  picks from the same order for Jellyfin apps.
- **Remembered per show.** Pick an audio track or subtitles while watching a show, and its
  other episodes start on the same choice. This is on by default as **Remember picks per show**,
  and it is saved to your account, so it follows you to other devices.
- **The version's default track.** **Play the version's default track first** decides whether
  a track the file marks as its default wins over your languages. Turn it off and your
  languages always win.
- **Your own subtitle file.** Drop a subtitle file on the player, or pick one with **Add a
  file** in the subtitle menu.
- **Real tracks.** Files AIOStreams has [read](#media-info-from-your-own-plays) list their
  actual tracks, rather than one numbered track per language.

### Who is watching

- **Ask every time.** Turn on **Ask when the app opens** under **Settings → Account → Who's
  watching**, and the app opens on its user picker, and again when you come back to it after
  half an hour away. A user with a PIN is always asked for it at those times, so a device left
  signed in doesn't stay open to anyone. While the server can't be reached, the last user
  comes straight in, since no PIN could be checked.
- **A PIN on every switch.** Switching to a user with a PIN asks for it every time, even on a
  device that user signed in on.
- **Users outside your household.** Turn on **Keep separate from other users** for someone's
  user, and they get a sign-in address of their own, which signs in only that user and shows
  none of the others. [Users outside your household](/apps/media-server#users-outside-your-household)
  explains it.

### Your choice of player

**Settings → Playback → Player** decides where **Play** sends a version:

- **In a browser**, the app's own player, or an app on your device: VLC, Infuse, Outplayer or
  IINA, depending on what your device runs. Infuse and Outplayer bring you back with your place
  saved. **Custom link** takes your own player's link, with the stream address, file name,
  subtitles, title and start position to fill in.
- **In the Android app**, its own player, or VLC, MX Player, mpv or any other video app. They
  start where you left off and show the title, and the app saves your place when you come back.
- **In the desktop app**, **Built-in mpv** plays inside the app, and **Your own mpv** starts
  the mpv installed on your computer, in its own window with its own configuration and scripts.
  The app finds it where mpv is usually installed, or you choose it. The app's controls still
  drive it, your place and watched marks save, and the next episode plays in the same window.
  The Linux Flatpak can't start other programs, so it plays in the app only.

This replaces the **External player** setting and the **Open in external player** button on
each version. If you had every version opening in your external player, that carries over.

### Search suggestions

While you type a search, up to five titles show under the search box, and picking one
searches for it. In the Android app they also show above the keyboard, where a TV remote
reaches them without closing it. Jellyfin apps that show search hints as you type get the
same titles. They come from Cinemeta's popular titles unless your instance has
[LumiereDB](#running-lumieredb), which knows every IMDb title, copes with typos and finds
titles by their original names too.

### Calendar and Upcoming

The **Calendar** now has the shows and films on your watchlist, which are your favourites, as
well as the episodes of shows you watch.

### The desktop app

- **Your own mpv**, as [described above](#your-choice-of-player), and
  [downloads](#downloads-and-saved-pages).
- **Clearer errors.** When a stream won't load, the error says why: the link answered with an
  HTTP error such as 404, the server could not be reached, or the link returned something
  other than a video.

### Faster

- Titles open sooner. A title's page draws from the copy the list already had while the rest
  loads, and a show's seasons and next episode are asked for as soon as its page starts to
  open.
- Home stays loaded while other pages and the player are open, so going back to it is quick.
- Scrolling and moving between cards are smoother on TVs and touch screens, with artwork
  resized away from the page.
- Next Up and Continue Watching load faster with a long watch history.

### Smaller things

- **Live updates.** Progress and played marks from another device show up without a refresh.
- **The server list** shows each server's logo, who is signed in, or that it can't be reached.
- **Up to 25 versions.** The media server offers up to 25 versions of a title by default, up
  from 10. Set your own number under **Versions per item** on the **Playback** tab.
- Subtitles move up out of the way while the player's controls show.
- In Jellyfin for Android, its player plays on to the next episode by itself.
- Remove a recent search from its menu.

## Media info from your own plays

AIOStreams used to know a file's tracks only when an addon or StremThru said what they were.
Now it reads them itself. The first time a file plays, AIOStreams reads its start with
FFmpeg's ffprobe and keeps its audio and subtitle tracks, video format, HDR and languages for
everyone on the instance. The next time that file turns up in a stream list, from any addon,
it carries what was read:

- **Filters, sorting and the formatter** see the real languages, audio formats and tracks, in
  Stremio as well as the apps.
- **The apps** list the file's real audio and subtitle tracks, not one numbered track per
  language.
- **Audio tracks say whether they are Atmos or DTS:X**, and a Dolby Vision file is tagged with
  the HDR10 or HDR10+ layer it also carries, so only a file without one counts as **DV Only**.
  Video with a bit depth of 10 gets the `10bit` tag.

Files are read when they play through the built-in usenet service, through TorBox, or through
the proxy set up under **Proxy**, whether from Stremio or the apps, and when an NZB is added
to the usenet library from the dashboard. Other debrid files are never read, since reading them
from the server could add another IP address to your debrid account.

AIOStreams also keeps what StremThru reports when a debrid service checks what it has cached.
A torrent StremThru has seen then carries its tracks for every addon that finds it, not just
the built-in addons.

In the apps, the first play of a file that hasn't been read yet still shows placeholder tracks.
**Wait for tracks on play**, on the media server's **Playback** tab, reads the file first and
waits up to that many seconds, so the first play lists every track too. Some apps give up after
about 20 seconds, so stay below that.

**RemuxDB lookups are now on by default.** The RemuxDB lookup, which fills in media info from
RemuxDB's database of probed files, runs for every configuration that hasn't turned it off
under **Filters → Miscellaneous**. It runs alongside the addon search, so it doesn't add
to the wait. Anything this instance has read itself, or kept from StremThru, comes first.
[Upgrading](#upgrading) says what a lookup sends and how to turn it off. What your instance
reads can go to RemuxDB too, if you [contribute](#contribute-to-remuxdb).

The probe builds on work by [webstreamr](https://github.com/webstreamr).

### Atmos and DTS:X in the formatter

An audio track now has `tags`, a list of its format followed by Atmos or DTS:X when the track
has them. It replaces `tag`, which still works but only holds the format.

```
{stream.audioTracks::where('tags=Atmos')::exists["Atmos"||""]}
{stream.audioTracks::each("{track.lang::languageCode} {track.tags::join(' ')} {track.channels}")::join(' | ')}

  ->  EN TrueHD Atmos 7.1 | EN DD 2.0
```

In stream expressions, `audioTrack(streams, 'lang=English', 'tags=Atmos')` matches streams
with an English Atmos track.

## For addon developers

- **More stream details.** AIOStreams reads `service`, `cached`, `seeders`, `indexer`,
  `publishDate` and `duration` from a stream's `behaviorHints`, ahead of anything it would
  parse from the name and description. With `duration`, it works out the bitrate too. The new
  [Streams](/reference/addon-protocol/streams) page lists every field.
- **Filler and recap marks.** Set `filler` or `recap` to `true` on a video in your meta, and
  the AIOStreams app marks the episode and passes over it when playing on. AIOMetadata's
  `isFiller` and `isRecap` are read too. See [Metadata](/reference/addon-protocol/metadata).
- **Named trailers.** A `trailers` entry can carry a `name`, which the apps show, and its
  `source` can be a video's address, which they play as it is, ahead of YouTube trailers.
  `trailerStreams` are still read. See [Metadata](/reference/addon-protocol/metadata#trailers).
- **Watch State.** A pulled watchlist or rating entry can carry `kind`, `movie` or `series`,
  for meta types that don't say which, such as `anime`. A pulled Next Up entry can carry
  `airsAt`, so an episode that hasn't aired yet waits until it has. See
  [Watch State](/reference/addon-protocol/watch-state).

## For self-hosters

### Contribute to RemuxDB

RemuxDB is a shared database of probed files, and AIOStreams instances look files up in it.
Now that AIOStreams reads the files you play, your instance can add what it reads, so the
next person who finds that release sees its real tracks without anyone playing it first. The
more instances contribute, the more of every stream list RemuxDB fills in, on self-hosted and
public instances alike.

Contributing is off unless you turn it on, with **Settings → RemuxDB → Contribute to RemuxDB**
or `REMUXDB_CONTRIBUTE=true`. Your instance then sends RemuxDB the tracks it reads from played
files that RemuxDB doesn't have yet. Each submission names the torrent's info hash, or the
indexer and the release id on it, along with the file name, the title it was played for and a
random id for your instance. It is sent after a file has been read, so it never slows down a
stream list.

### The Media info page

![The Media info page on a busy instance, with 693K files with tracks, 24% of streams filled in over the last day, the Sources and Probing cards, and a list of probed files with their resolution, track counts and size](/changelog/v2.36/media-info.webp)

**Dashboard → Media info** shows how many files have tracks stored, how many streams they
filled in over the last day, and how many probes ran and how long they took. It breaks the
stored files down by source: probed here, kept from StremThru or RemuxDB, or looked up on
another instance. Below that is the probe queue, where a probe can be cancelled, every stored
file with its tracks, and every probe from the last 30 days with how it ended. In the usenet
library, each NZB shows how many of its files have been read.

Probing is on by default. It reads one file at a time, and each file stored takes a few
hundred bytes in the database.

### Sharing media info between instances

An instance can let others look up what it has stored: **Share with other instances**
(`MEDIA_INFO_SERVE`, off). Another instance then points **Look up on another instance**
(`MEDIA_INFO_LOOKUP_URL`) at it, and files with nothing stored locally are looked up there.
Both are under **Settings → Media Info**.

Sharing is read-only, and only answers with what it has: the tracks of files played on that
instance, looked up by info hash or NZB key. That does show which releases have been played
there, and the instance that looks up sends the info hashes and NZB keys of every stream list's
results, so only pair instances that trust each other.

### Running LumiereDB

[Search suggestions](#search-suggestions) can come from LumiereDB, a self-hosted index of
IMDb's datasets. Add it to the same compose file as AIOStreams, so AIOStreams reaches it by
its container name:

```yaml
services:
  lumiere-db:
    image: ghcr.io/0xconstant1/lumiere-db:latest
    container_name: lumiere-db
    restart: unless-stopped
    user: '${PUID}:${PGID}'
    stop_grace_period: 75s
    expose:
      - 8000
    environment:
      - CACHE_MAX_BYTES=256MB
      - SUGGEST_ENABLED=true
    volumes:
      - ${DOCKER_DATA_DIR}/lumiere-db:/data
```

Create the data folder as your own user before the first start, or Docker creates it as root
and LumiereDB can't write to it. The first start downloads about 2 GB from IMDb and builds its
index, which takes a while, and it needs about 5 GB of disk. Then set **LumiereDB URL** to
`http://lumiere-db:8000` and **Search suggestions** to `lumieredb`, both under **Settings →
Media server**. IMDb's datasets are for personal, non-commercial use.
[Search suggestions](/apps/media-server#search-suggestions) has the details.

### New settings

- **Media info**, under **Settings → Media Info**. **Probe played files** (`MEDIA_INFO_PROBE`,
  on) turns probing on. **Probe on** (`MEDIA_INFO_PROBE_ON`, `stremio,jellyfin,library`) sets
  where: `shares` adds the first read through the WebDAV, NFS and FUSE shares, which Sonarr,
  Radarr and media server scans usually probe themselves. **Store media info from**
  (`MEDIA_INFO_BACKFILL`, `stremthru`) can add `remuxdb`. **Wait for tracks on play**
  (`MEDIA_INFO_PLAY_WAIT`, 0) is the default for configurations that set none. **Max
  concurrent probes** (`MEDIA_INFO_MAX_CONCURRENT_PROBES`, 1), **Max queued probes**
  (`MEDIA_INFO_MAX_QUEUED_PROBES`, 50) and **Probe timeout** (`MEDIA_INFO_PROBE_TIMEOUT`,
  90 seconds) bound the work, and **ffprobe path** (`FFPROBE_PATH`) points at ffprobe if it is
  not on the PATH.
- **Max versions per item**, under **Settings → Media server** (`JELLYFIN_MAX_VERSIONS`), now
  defaults to 25 and goes up to 500.
- **Search suggestions**, under **Settings → Media server** (`JELLYFIN_SEARCH_SUGGESTIONS`,
  `cinemeta`). `cinemeta` matches Cinemeta's list of about 20,000 popular movies and series,
  which the server downloads and keeps in memory. `lumieredb` asks the instance in **LumiereDB
  URL** (`JELLYFIN_LUMIEREDB_URL`), see [Running LumiereDB](#running-lumieredb). `catalogs`
  runs each configuration's own catalog search, which knows what its addons do but costs as
  much as a full search.

## Upgrading

Nothing in this release changes install URLs or needs a manual migration. A few things are
worth checking.

**ffprobe.** The Docker image includes a small ffprobe. Running from source, install FFmpeg's
ffprobe, version 7 or newer to tell DTS:X apart, or set **ffprobe path**. Without it nothing is
probed, and the Media info page says so.

**RemuxDB lookups are on for every configuration.** Each lookup sends the title's IMDb ID, with
the season and episode, from your instance. A configuration that turned **RemuxDB
Integration** off keeps it off. **Settings → RemuxDB → RemuxDB** (`REMUXDB_ENABLED`) turns
lookups and contributions off for the whole instance.

**More versions.** With **Settings → Media server → Max versions per item**
(`JELLYFIN_MAX_VERSIONS`) now at 25, configurations that never set **Versions per item** offer
up to 25 versions instead of 10. Set it back to 10 to keep the old limit.

**`tag` on audio tracks.** `{track.tag}` in the formatter, and `tag=` in `audioTrack()`, still
work, but `tags` replaces them and also holds Atmos and DTS:X. Templates that show
`{track.tag}` keep showing the format alone.

**Search hints.** Jellyfin's search hints, which some Jellyfin apps show as you type, came from
each configuration's catalog search and now come from Cinemeta's list of popular titles. Set
**Settings → Media server → Search suggestions** to `catalogs` to keep the old behaviour.

**AIO Subtitle and Argentina TV are removed.** Neither addon is hosted any more. A
configuration that still has one skips it and says why. AIO Subtitle's developer has moved to
a new version at aiosubtitle.org.

**What is on by default.** Probing played files, keeping what StremThru reports, RemuxDB
lookups, and search suggestions from Cinemeta's list, which the server downloads from
Cinemeta the first time someone searches, and again at most every 8 hours.
[New settings](#new-settings) says where to switch each one off. Sharing media info with
other instances and [contributing to RemuxDB](#contribute-to-remuxdb) are off.
