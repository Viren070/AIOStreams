v2.38 brings **Live TV** to every AIOStreams app and makes the app in a browser a **full player**.
The apps also upscale anime as they play, play trailers by themselves, work properly with any
Jellyfin server and come in 12 more languages. And AIOStreams has a new home at
[aiostreams.org](https://aiostreams.org).

What's in it:

- **[Live TV](#live-tv)**: the channels from your IPTV provider or your addons, with a programme
  guide, catch-up and reminders, and a new **Xtream** addon for your provider's login.
- **[A full player in your browser](#a-full-player-in-your-browser)**: HEVC, Dolby Vision, TrueHD
  and DTS, audio tracks, a file's own subtitles, and styled subtitles drawn as their authors made
  them.
- **[Anime upscaling](#anime-upscaling)**: sharper lines and cleaner compression, as it plays.
- **[Trailers that play by themselves](#trailers-that-play-by-themselves)** in cards, the featured
  title and behind a title's page.
- **[Any Jellyfin server](#with-any-jellyfin-server)**: transcoding, the server's chapters and seek
  previews, sorting and filtering.
- **[12 more languages](#the-apps-in-your-language)**, translated by the community.
- **[Who gets this](#who-gets-this)** on the configure page: give an addon or catalog to some of
  your users without writing a variant.
- **[More in the app](#more-in-the-app)**: a mini player, picture-in-picture, a background from
  the title's artwork, a rating from each source, chapters with pictures, a pause screen and more.
- **[Around AIOStreams](#around-aiostreams)**: the docs at aiostreams.org, the app at
  web.aiostreams.org, and Watch Import for moving from Stremio or Nuvio.

Self-hosters should read [Upgrading](#upgrading): instances now ask search engines not to list
them, and variants share one work budget in place of six limits.

## Live TV

Live TV puts the channels from every addon in your configuration on one page, grouped, numbered and
searchable, with a guide of what's on. It's in every AIOStreams app: in a browser, on computers,
phones and tablets, and on TVs. Once your configuration has channels, **Live TV** shows in the
navigation.

![Live TV in the desktop app: the programme guide, with the playing channel in a preview above it and its programme's details beside it](/changelog/v2.38/live-tv-guide.webp)

Channels come from addons, like everything else in AIOStreams:

- **Your IPTV provider.** The new **Xtream** addon, in the configure page's marketplace, signs in
  with the server address, username and password your provider gave you. It brings the provider's
  channels, their groups and their guide, and earlier programmes where the provider keeps them.
- **Free channels**, from marketplace addons such as **USA TV** and **Debridio TV**.
- **Any addon with channels**, added by its URL, such as one that reads an M3U playlist and an XMLTV
  guide.

On the Live TV page:

- **The guide.** **Guide** and **Channels** switch between the programme guide and a plain list, on
  screens wide enough for a guide. **Days** step through it: today, tomorrow, and earlier days for
  channels with catch-up.
- **Groups.** **Favourites** and **Recent** come first, then **All channels** and the groups your
  addons send. With many groups, a button opens them in a list you can search.
- **Channel numbers.** Type a number on a keyboard or remote to go to that channel. Channels keep
  the numbers their addon gives them, or are numbered in order.
- **Watching.** A channel plays in a preview at the top of the page, so you can keep looking
  through the guide. **Full screen** opens the player, where channel up and down, or Page Up and
  Page Down, change channel and **Previous channel** goes back to the last one. Pause and you fall
  behind live; **Go live** catches up. A channel that drops is reopened by itself.
- **Programmes.** Select one for its details: **From the start** plays the programme on now from
  its beginning, and **Remind me** asks, when a later one starts, whether to switch to it.
- **Catch-up.** Where a provider keeps the last few days of a channel, its past programmes play
  from the start, as a recording you can pause and seek.
- **Favourites** go in their own group, on the **Favourites** page and in a row on Home, and
  **Start on** under **Settings → Interface** can open the app on Live TV.
- **Search** finds channels by name or number, next to films and shows.

The page reopens on the channel you were last on. Live channels start without subtitles, and the
audio and subtitles you pick are kept for each channel.

![Live TV in the Android app on a TV: the channel list beside a card for the focused channel, with the channel playing in its preview](/changelog/v2.38/live-tv-tv.webp)

![Two phones: Live TV's channel list with a channel playing at the top, and Home with a row of favourite channels and the mini player](/changelog/v2.38/live-tv-phones.webp)

The same channels show in Stremio, where the guide fills Stremio's own, and Jellyfin apps get them
as Live TV too. [Live TV](/apps/live-tv) has the details. The channels shown are made up, playing

## A full player in your browser

The app in a browser, from your instance's `/web` or at [web.aiostreams.org](#around-aiostreams),
used to play only what the browser decodes itself. MKV files, HEVC video and surround sound often
failed, there was no way to switch audio tracks, a file's own subtitles didn't show, and styled
subtitles came out as plain text. Now the player reads and decodes the file itself with the new
**WebGPU engine**, and draws it with the graphics card, so a browser plays much like the apps:

- **More files play**: HEVC and Dolby Vision video, and AC-3, E-AC-3, TrueHD and DTS sound, in MKV
  and MP4 files.
- **Audio tracks.** The audio menu switches between a file's tracks, for another language or a
  commentary.
- **The file's own subtitles.** The subtitle tracks inside an MKV or MP4 file show in the subtitle
  menu: ASS, SRT, WebVTT, MP4's own text subtitles, and PGS, the picture subtitles on Blu-rays.
- **Styled subtitles as their authors made them.** ASS and SSA subtitles are drawn with libass, the
  renderer mpv uses, so their styling, positions, signs, karaoke, animations and the fonts attached
  to the file look as they do in the desktop and Android apps.
- **Automatic subtitle syncing.** A subtitle from your addons, or a file you add, is synced to the
  file's own subtitles by itself, and one made for another frame rate is retimed first.
- **Picture-in-picture** with the player's subtitles and controls. See
  [Mini player and picture-in-picture](#mini-player-and-picture-in-picture).
- **Anime upscaling and your own shaders.** See [Anime upscaling](#anime-upscaling).
- **Live channels and other HLS streams** play in every browser, not only those that play HLS
  themselves.
- **A stats panel** on **I**.

None of it needs setting up. On a screen with HDR, turn on **HDR output** under **Settings →
Playback → Player** to show HDR video in HDR, instead of converted to standard range.

![Sintel playing in a browser with the WebGPU engine: a styled sign reading The Land of the Gatekeepers over the picture, a subtitle line below it, and the audio menu open on the file's AC-3 5.1 and DTS 5.1 tracks](/changelog/v2.38/browser.webp)

### When the browser's own player plays

The WebGPU engine needs a browser with WebGPU, such as Chrome, Edge or Firefox, on a page opened
over HTTPS. It also needs the file's host to let the browser read the file: streams through
AIOStreams' proxy or its usenet streaming do, and so does TorBox.

Otherwise, and for live channels and streams that need extra headers, the browser's own player
plays as before. It still draws styled subtitles with libass, and shows a file's own subtitle tracks
where the host allows. If the engine can't play a version, as it opens or partway through, the
browser's player takes over at the same point, with the same audio and subtitles. **Engine** under
**Settings → Playback → Player** switches to the browser's own player for everything.

## Anime upscaling

The desktop and Android apps, and the [WebGPU engine](#a-full-player-in-your-browser) in a browser,
can upscale anime as it plays: lines come out sharper and compression cleaner, most of all in older
and lower-resolution shows. Turn on **Anime upscaling** under **Settings → Playback → Video**.

- **Only anime.** It applies to the shows and films your catalogs mark as anime, and on other
  Jellyfin servers to titles with an Anime genre or tag. Everything else plays as it was.
- **On the graphics processor.** It leaves HDR video, and video about as large as the screen, as
  they are, and turns itself off for a video the device can't keep up with. On Android it works in
  both ExoPlayer and mpv, and uses more battery.
- **Compare it.** In the desktop app and a browser, **U** turns it on and off for what's playing.
- **Or bring your own.** The Android app's built-in mpv now takes your own shaders, with **Add a
  shader** under **Settings → Android app**, as the desktop app's mpv folder already does. In a
  browser, **Shaders** under **Settings → Playback → Player** takes `.wgsl` files; they stay in
  that browser and apply in name order. mpv's GLSL shaders need converting first.

## Trailers that play by themselves

![Home on a TV, with the Project Hail Mary card in the Popular row opened out to a wide picture, its trailer playing under its logo, scores and synopsis, and the posters after it moved along](/changelog/v2.38/trailer-card.webp)

Rest on a title for a moment and its trailer plays:

- **In cards.** A moment after a card on Home opens out, its trailer plays in it, and its details
  fade away. Move on and it stops. See [Cards you rest on](#home) for how cards open.
- **In the featured title.** When the featured title follows the card you're on, or sits in the
  corner, the card's trailer plays in a wide box at the top of Home. When it rotates, a title's
  trailer plays once you've gone to it yourself: up from the rows, turning to it, or picking its
  dot.
- **Behind a title's page.** Left at the top of a title's page for a few seconds, its trailer plays
  behind the page. **Up**, **Playback controls** or the page's trailer button shows it whole with
  the player's controls, carrying on from where it was, and **Back** returns to the page with it
  still playing.

![Project Hail Mary's page on a TV with its trailer playing behind it, Playing and the trailer's name in the corner, and Press Up for playback controls at the top](/changelog/v2.38/trailer-title.webp)

**Play by themselves** under **Settings → Interface** picks where they play. They're on with a
mouse and on TVs, and off on phones and tablets. **Sound** beside it plays them with sound, on by
default only on TVs, and a trailer you open whole always has sound. On Samsung TVs they play by
themselves only with Sound on. They wait while something plays, during a watch party, and when your
device asks for reduced motion or your browser is saving data.

## With any Jellyfin server

The AIOStreams app signs in to Jellyfin servers as well as to your configuration, and now works
properly with Jellyfin's own:

- **Transcoding.** When the player can't play a version, the server converts it. **Quality** under
  **Settings → Playback**, and a quality menu in the player, set the most a stream may take, for a
  slow connection. Both show where your account may transcode; AIOStreams itself never transcodes.
- **Chapters and seek previews** come from the server's chapters and trickplay pictures.
- **Sorting and filtering.** A Jellyfin server's libraries sort by name, date added, release date,
  rating or runtime, and filter to what's in progress, watched or a favourite. **Favourites** sorts
  by date added, name, release date or rating.
- **Search** finds collections and episodes too.
- **One version plays at once.** A title with a single version, as most in a Jellyfin library,
  plays without the version list.
- **Home** shows each library's newest additions first.
- **Your account's permissions.** Downloads and settings follow what the server lets your account
  do.

With [web.aiostreams.org](#around-aiostreams), a browser is all you need to use the app with a
Jellyfin server.

## The apps in your language

The AIOStreams apps now come in **12 languages besides English**: Chinese (Simplified), French,
German, Greek, Indonesian, Italian, Malay, Polish, Portuguese, Portuguese (Brazil), Spanish and
Turkish. Choose yours under **Settings → Interface → Language**, or leave it on **System** to
follow the device.

A language shows in the apps once at least half of it is translated, so a half-done translation
doesn't leave you switching between languages. Dutch, Swedish, Norwegian, Finnish, Slovenian and
Catalan have been started and need help. Translations are made on
[Hosted Weblate](https://hosted.weblate.org/engage/aiostreams/), with no coding needed, and the
[translating guide](/guides/translating) explains the rest.

## More in the app

Everything here works in every app, in a browser too, unless it says otherwise.

### Mini player and picture-in-picture

**Mini player** in the player keeps the video going in a corner while you look around the app.
Select it to open the player again, or set **Back in the player** under **Settings → Playback →
Player** to open it with **Back**. It's in every app except on TVs, where Live TV's preview is the
small player.

![Home in the desktop app with Project Hail Mary featured, and the mini player in the bottom corner playing Sintel, with its title, a pause button and a close button below the video](/changelog/v2.38/mini-player.webp)

**Picture-in-picture**, a new button in the player's controls, moves the video into a small window
that stays over your other apps:

- **In a browser.** In Chrome, Edge and Firefox, the whole player moves into the window, with its
  subtitles, controls and seek bar. In Chrome and Edge, **Picture-in-picture** under **Settings →
  Playback → Player** can use the browser's own window of the video alone instead, which is what
  Safari shows.
- **In the desktop app on Windows**, the app's window shrinks to the video, with its own controls,
  keeps the video's shape as you resize it, and opens where you left it.
- **In the Android app** on phones and tablets, the button does what leaving the app already did.

### Background from artwork

Turn on **Background from artwork** under **Settings → Theme**, and the artwork of the title you're
on sits behind the pages, blurred and darkened: a title's page, the featured title on Home, or a
card you rest on. It stays behind every page, Settings too, until you get to another title, and
fades from one title's to the next.

![The Rings of Power's page in the desktop app with Background from artwork on: its picture fades into a blurred, darkened brown behind the episodes, and an episode's menu on frosted glass shows the picture under it](/changelog/v2.38/art-background.webp)

- **Panels to match.** Menus, lists, fields and cards over it take their colour from the artwork.
- **Glass panels.** With **Glass surface** set to frosted or liquid glass, **Glass panels** puts
  them on that glass instead, so the artwork shows through. Slower devices can feel the extra
  drawing.
- **Trailers** playing behind the featured title or a title's page fade into it at their edges.
- **One colour instead.** [Custom CSS](/reference/web-app-css#one-colour-from-the-artwork) can swap
  the blurred artwork for its colour alone.

### Home

![Home in the desktop app with Modern Family featured, Play and More info below its summary, and the Severance card in Continue watching grown into a preview with its episode, air date, time left and synopsis](/changelog/v2.38/card-preview.webp)

- **Play for shows too.** The rotating featured title now has **Play** for shows as well as films,
  where a show used to have only **More info**.
- **Time on each title**, under **Settings → Interface**, sets how long the featured title stays on
  each, and a mouse can now drag it along, as a swipe does.
- **Cards you rest on.** While the featured title rotates, a card you rest on with a remote or the
  keyboard opens out in its row to a wide picture with its details, and the cards after it slide
  along. With a mouse it pops out a bigger preview over the row instead, so the cards under the
  pointer stay put. Cards in grids, such as a catalog's page, pop out too. **Card you rest on**
  under **Settings → Interface** picks which, or turns them off.
- **Favourite channels** get a row of their own.

### Ratings from each source

Title pages show a badge for each score your metadata addon sends: IMDb, Rotten Tomatoes' critics
and audience, Metacritic, Letterboxd, RogerEbert.com, Trakt, TMDB and MyAnimeList. Where room is
short, as on Home, the first three show. AIOMetadata sends them once its critic ratings are on, and
[custom CSS](/reference/web-app-css) can hide the ones you don't want.

![Severance's page in the desktop app, with its IMDb, Rotten Tomatoes, Metacritic and Trakt scores beside its age rating, above its summary and Resume](/changelog/v2.38/title.webp)

### Title pages

- **Episodes** show their title first, with their air date and a menu button, and a bar slides to
  the season that's open.
- **Unaired episodes** are marked as such. The app asks before playing or downloading one, and
  passes over them when playing on or downloading a season. **Mark unaired episodes** under
  **Settings → Playback** turns it off, for shows whose air dates are often wrong.
- **No spoilers, built in.** Blurring the episodes you haven't started used to take custom CSS. Now
  **Blur episodes you haven't started**, under **Settings → Interface**, blurs their pictures,
  titles and descriptions until you're on one.
- **Every version.** The app lists up to 500 versions of a title, where it used to stop at your
  configuration's **Versions per item**, which now applies only to Jellyfin apps.

### In the player

- **A pause screen.** Left paused for a few seconds, the player dims and shows what's playing: the
  title, the episode and its synopsis.
- **Chapters with pictures.** In the desktop and Android apps, **Chapters** lists a file's chapters
  in a sheet, each with a picture.
- **With a TV remote**, a press of left or right skips, and holding it scrubs through a row of
  pictures of the video. A remote's channel buttons go to the next or previous chapter.
- **Select** plays or pauses while the controls are hidden. Turn off **Show the controls on pause
  and skip**, under **Settings → Playback**, to keep them hidden then.
- **Subtitles glide.** When the controls show, subtitles now slide smoothly up above them and back
  down after, instead of jumping.
- **Styled subtitles from addons.** In the desktop and Android apps, ASS subtitles from your addons
  keep their styling, where before they arrived as plain text.
- **The time** shows in the player's top bar.
- **Find versions when a title opens**, under **Settings → Playback**, looks for versions as soon
  as a title's page opens, so **Play** doesn't wait.

- **Tabs along the top.** On a computer or a tablet held sideways, the tabs can run along the top
  instead of down the side, under **Settings → Interface**. They, and the bar at the bottom of a

- **Start on**, under **Settings → Interface**, picks the page the app opens on.
- **The bottom bar** slides away while you scroll down, and back as you scroll up.
- **Liquid glass** now bends what's behind it like a lens, with a lit rim. The navigation's

### LG and Samsung TVs

The TV apps keep the TV's own player, and get the browser's new subtitles:

- **Styled subtitles as their authors made them**, drawn with libass instead of as plain text. Very
  heavy typesetting can drop frames on a slower TV.
- **The file's own subtitles.** The subtitle tracks inside an MKV or MP4 file show in the subtitle
  menu, from any host, PGS included.
- **Automatic subtitle syncing** to the file's own subtitles, as in the other apps.
- **Trailers** now play, in the TV's own player, and
  [by themselves](#trailers-that-play-by-themselves) as in the other apps.

### The Android app

- **Styled text subtitles in ExoPlayer.** ExoPlayer now draws SRT and other text subtitles with
  libass too, so they look the same in both engines and slide smoothly.
- **Smoother menus.** On a screen with a high refresh rate, the controls and menus over the video
  move at its fastest rate, while the video itself keeps its own.
- **Opening the app** shows its logo until the app is ready, instead of a blank screen.
- **Fuller reports.** **Report a problem** sends more of the app's log, and after a crash, what the
  app was doing just before.

### Smaller things

- **Playlists** can **Shuffle**, in a mixed order that stays the same until you turn it off, and
  **Repeat**, going back to the first title after the last.
- **Quick Connect.** **Quick Connect** under **Settings → Account** signs another device in as you,
  with the code it shows.
- **Time format** under **Settings → Interface** shows times of day, such as the player's clock and
  the TV guide, in a 12- or 24-hour clock. On **Automatic**, the desktop and Android apps follow the
  device's own setting.
- **Faster on TVs.** Rows scroll with less work, artwork loads nearest first, at the size it's
  drawn, and the player's controls show and hide with about a third of the work they took.
- **Licences.** **About** lists the open source projects in the app, with their licences.
- **More to style with custom CSS.** The new pages, the mini player, the player's top bar and
  chapters, and the episode states have hooks. See [Web app CSS](/reference/web-app-css).

## The configure page

### Who gets this

Giving an addon or catalog to only some of the people who use your configuration used to mean
writing [variant](/reference/config-expressions) lines by hand. Now the people button beside each
addon in **My Addons**, and each catalog in **Catalogs**, opens **Who gets this**, with a switch for:

- **Each user** of your media server, such as a separate setup for the kids.
- **Each variant you install from a link**, and the **base config** for install links without one.

Turn one off and the configure page writes the variant lines for you. They stay yours to read and
change under **Miscellaneous → Variants**, and
[who gets an addon or catalog](/reference/config-expressions#who-gets-an-addon-or-catalog) explains
the lines it writes.

![The Who gets Library dialog on the configure page: switches for the users Alex, the primary user, Jordan, Sam and Kids, with Kids turned off, and for the base config under Install links](/changelog/v2.38/who-gets-this.webp)

### Variants

- **Back-to-back selectors** narrow the same list, so
  `presets[type=torrentio][options.name*="4K"]` is only the Torrentio instances with 4K in their
  name.
- **Moving entries.** `move` reorders a list without writing its entries out, so
  `move presets[type=comet] before [0]` puts Comet first. An addon or catalog moved this way keeps
  its options, API keys included, out of the script, where a pasted copy would go stale. See
  [moving entries](/reference/config-expressions#moving-entries).
- **One work budget.** The variants on a request share a budget of work in place of six separate
  limits, and an instance allows 50 variants by default, up from 10. See [Upgrading](#upgrading).

### Also on the configure page

- **The order of users.** Drag users in the **Users** tab into the order Jellyfin apps' sign-in
  pickers list them in. The primary user stays first.
- **Your own poster service.** **Custom URL** under **Services → Posters** takes a poster address
  of your own, with placeholders such as `{imdb_id}` for each title's id.
- **New in the marketplace**: a **Cinemeta** addon, and **SearchNZB** among the Newznab indexers.

## The media server

For Jellyfin apps signed in to your configuration:

- **Live TV.** The channels, their groups and the guide show as Jellyfin's Live TV, and past
  programmes with catch-up play from their start.
- **Ratings.** A title's IMDb score is its community rating and its Rotten Tomatoes score its
  critic rating, from your addons' [ratings](#ratings-from-each-source).
- **Played at the right point.** The AIOStreams apps send the length of the file they played, so a
  title is marked played at the right point even when its runtime from metadata is minutes off.

## For addon developers

- **Live TV.** A catalog of type `tv` or `channel` is a list of channels, with `genre` for their
  groups, and a guide in Stremio's own format fills the programme guide, in Stremio too. Channel
  numbers, logos and catch-up are covered in [Live TV](/reference/addon-protocol/live-tv).
- **Ratings.** `ratings` on a meta lists each source's score, in the order to show them. See
  [Metadata](/reference/addon-protocol/metadata#ratings).
- **An addon SDK.** [`@aiostreams/addon-sdk`](https://github.com/AIOStreams/addon-sdk) handles the
  routing, the configuration in the URL, a configure page built from your settings, and checks of
  every answer, on Node, Bun, Deno and Cloudflare Workers. The new
  [addon development guide](/guides/addon-development) walks through building one, trying it in
  AIOStreams and hosting it.
- **A tracker to learn from.** [Watch Import](https://github.com/Viren070/watch-import) is a
  [Watch State](/reference/addon-protocol/watch-state) tracker built with the SDK: it reads your
  history from Stremio or Nuvio and hands it to AIOStreams.

## Around AIOStreams

- **aiostreams.org.** The docs, this changelog and the app downloads moved from
  docs.aiostreams.viren070.me to [aiostreams.org](https://aiostreams.org). Old links still work and
  take you to the same page.
- **The app at web.aiostreams.org.** [web.aiostreams.org](https://web.aiostreams.org) hosts the
  AIOStreams app, so you can use it in any browser, even without your instance's own `/web`. Add
  your configuration's media server or a Jellyfin server and sign in, and install it from the
  browser to open it like an app. The server needs an `https://` address: a browser won't reach a
  plain `http://` one from a secure page.
- **Watch Import.** Coming from Stremio or Nuvio? [Watch Import](https://watch-import.viren070.me)
  is a tracker addon that brings your watch history, Continue Watching and library into AIOStreams
  and keeps them up to date. Add it to your configuration like any other addon. Its source is on
  [GitHub](https://github.com/Viren070/watch-import).

## For self-hosters

### The Xtream addon

The Xtream addon's settings are under **Settings → Built-ins**: how long a request may take
(`BUILTIN_XTREAM_TIMEOUT`), its user agent (`BUILTIN_XTREAM_USER_AGENT`), how many requests go to
one provider at once (`BUILTIN_XTREAM_HOST_CONCURRENCY`), how often channel lists and guides are
fetched again (`BUILTIN_XTREAM_LINEUP_TTL`, `BUILTIN_XTREAM_GUIDE_TTL`), and how much memory each
may use (`BUILTIN_XTREAM_LINEUP_MEMORY`, `BUILTIN_XTREAM_GUIDE_MEMORY`).

### New settings

- **Allow search engine indexing**, under **Settings → Branding** (`ALLOW_SEARCH_INDEXING`, off).
  See [Upgrading](#upgrading).
- **Max variant work per request**, under **Settings → User Limits** (`MAX_VARIANT_WORK`,
  100000), in place of six variant limits. See [Upgrading](#upgrading).
- **Max versions in the app**, under **Settings → Media server** (`JELLYFIN_MAX_APP_VERSIONS`,
  500), how many versions the AIOStreams app lists. **Max versions per item**
  (`JELLYFIN_MAX_VERSIONS`) now applies only to Jellyfin apps.
- **Cinemeta**'s address, timeout and user agent, under **Settings → Presets** (`CINEMETA_URL`,
  `DEFAULT_CINEMETA_TIMEOUT`, `DEFAULT_CINEMETA_USER_AGENT`).

## Upgrading

Nothing in this release changes install URLs or needs a manual migration. A few defaults change.

**Search engines.** Every page an instance serves now asks search engines not to list it, with an
`X-Robots-Tag: noindex` header, so your configure page drops out of search results over the next
weeks. For a public instance you want people to find, turn on **Allow search engine indexing**
under **Settings → Branding** (`ALLOW_SEARCH_INDEXING`).

**Variant limits.** Variants on one request now share a work budget, **Max variant work per
request** (`MAX_VARIANT_WORK`, 100000), where each instruction costs one plus one for every entry
it looks through and every value it writes. It replaces `MAX_VARIANT_SCRIPT_LENGTH`,
`MAX_VARIANT_TOTAL_SCRIPT_CHARACTERS`, `MAX_VARIANT_TOTAL_INSTRUCTIONS`, `MAX_VARIANT_PATH_MATCHES`,
`MAX_VARIANT_VALUE_DEPTH` and `MAX_VARIANT_PATH_SEGMENTS`, which no longer do anything; scripts have
no length limit of their own beyond `MAX_JSON_BODY_SIZE`. **Max variants** (`MAX_VARIANTS`) now
defaults to 50, up from 10.

**Resume points.** **Minimum resume percentage** under **Settings → Watch State**
(`WATCH_STATE_MIN_RESUME_PERCENT`) now defaults to 0, so every position you stop at is kept. Set it
back to 5 to leave titles stopped in their first moments out of Continue Watching.

**Unaired episodes in Jellyfin apps.** **Mark unaired episodes**, in the media server's settings
under **Save & Install**, is now off unless you turned it on. Jellyfin apps list upcoming episodes
as normal ones, since some hide unaired episodes altogether. The AIOStreams app still marks them and
asks before playing one.
