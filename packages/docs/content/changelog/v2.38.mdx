**Live TV** comes to every AIOStreams app: the channels from your IPTV provider or your addons on
one page, with a programme guide, catch-up and reminders. The new **Xtream** addon signs in to your
provider with the details it gave you.

The desktop and Android apps **upscale anime** as it plays, for sharper lines and cleaner
compression, and a **mini player** keeps the video going in a corner while you look around the
app. In a browser and on LG and Samsung TVs, **styled subtitles** now show as their authors made
them, a file's own subtitle tracks show, and subtitles line themselves up as they do in the apps.
An experimental **WebGPU engine** lets a browser play what it couldn't before, such as HEVC, Dolby
Vision, TrueHD and DTS, with anime upscaling too.

Title pages show **a rating from each source**, from IMDb and Rotten Tomatoes to Letterboxd and
Trakt. The featured title on Home gets **Play** and **More info**, and resting on a card opens a
**preview**. Phones get a centred look, a labelled row of actions and Android's back swipe,
computers and tablets can put the tabs **along the top**, and the player adds **chapters with
pictures** and a **pause screen**.

The apps now come in **12 languages besides English**, thanks to the people translating them.

On the configure page, **who gets this** gives an addon or catalog to some of your users without
writing a variant. Self-hosters should read [Upgrading](#upgrading): instances now ask search
engines not to list them, and variants share one work budget in place of six limits.

## Live TV

Live TV puts the channels from every addon in your configuration on one page, grouped, numbered and
searchable, with a guide of what's on. It's in every AIOStreams app: in a browser, on computers,
phones and tablets, and on TVs. Once your configuration has channels, **Live TV** shows in the
navigation.

![Live TV in the desktop app: the programme guide, with the playing channel in a preview above it and its programme's details beside it](/changelog/v2.38/live-tv-guide.webp)

Channels come from addons, like everything else in AIOStreams:

- **Your IPTV provider.** The new **Xtream** addon, in the configure page's marketplace, signs in
  with the server address, username and password your provider gave you. It brings the provider's
  channels, their groups and their guide, and earlier programmes where the provider keeps them.
- **Free channels**, from marketplace addons such as **USA TV** and **Debridio TV**.
- **Any addon with channels**, added by its URL, such as one that reads an M3U playlist and an XMLTV
  guide.

On the Live TV page:

- **The guide.** **Guide** and **Channels** switch between the programme guide and a plain list, on
  screens wide enough for a guide. **Days** step through it: today, tomorrow, and earlier days for
  channels with catch-up.
- **Groups.** **Favourites** and **Recent** come first, then **All channels** and the groups your
  addons send. With many groups, a button opens them in a list you can search.
- **Channel numbers.** Type a number on a keyboard or remote to go to that channel. Channels keep
  the numbers their addon gives them, or are numbered in order.
- **Watching.** A channel plays in a preview at the top of the page, so you can keep looking
  through the guide. **Full screen** opens the player, where channel up and down, or Page Up and
  Page Down, change channel and **Previous channel** goes back to the last one. Pause and you fall
  behind live; **Go live** catches up. A channel that drops is reopened by itself.
- **Programmes.** Select one for its details: **From the start** plays the programme on now from
  its beginning, and **Remind me** asks, when a later one starts, whether to switch to it.
- **Catch-up.** Where a provider keeps the last few days of a channel, its past programmes play
  from the start, as a recording you can pause and seek.
- **Favourites** go in their own group, on the **Favourites** page and in a row on Home, and
  **Start on** under **Settings → Interface** can open the app on Live TV.
- **Search** finds channels by name or number, next to films and shows.

The page reopens on the channel you were last on. Live channels start without subtitles, and the
audio and subtitles you pick are kept for each channel.

![Live TV in the Android app on a TV: the channel list beside a card for the focused channel, with the channel playing in its preview](/changelog/v2.38/live-tv-tv.webp)

![Two phones: Live TV's channel list with a channel playing at the top, and Home with a row of favourite channels and the mini player](/changelog/v2.38/live-tv-phones.webp)

The same channels show in Stremio, where the guide fills Stremio's own, and Jellyfin apps get them
as Live TV too. [Live TV](/apps/live-tv) has the details. The channels shown are made up, playing
_Sintel_ and _Tears of Steel_, © Blender Foundation, shared under
[CC BY 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/3.0/).

## New in the app

Everything here works in every app, in a browser too, unless it says otherwise.

### Anime upscaling

The desktop and Android apps, and the [WebGPU engine](#the-webgpu-engine) in a browser, can upscale
anime as it plays: lines come out sharper and compression cleaner, most of all in older and
lower-resolution shows. Turn on **Anime upscaling** under **Settings → Playback → Video**.

- **Only anime.** It applies to the shows and films your catalogs mark as anime, and leaves
  everything else as it was.
- **On the graphics processor.** It leaves HDR video, and video about as large as the screen, as
  they are, and turns itself off for a video the device can't keep up with. On Android it works in
  both ExoPlayer and mpv, and uses more battery.
- **Compare it.** In the desktop app and a browser, **U** turns it on and off for what's playing.
- **Or bring your own.** The Android app's built-in mpv now takes your own shaders, with **Add a
  shader** under **Settings → Android app**, as the desktop app's mpv folder already does. The
  WebGPU engine takes shaders as `.wgsl` files, under **Shaders** in **Settings → Playback →
  Player**; they stay in that browser and apply in name order. mpv's GLSL shaders need converting
  first.

### Mini player

**Mini player** in the player keeps the video going in a corner while you look around the app.
Select it to open the player again. Set **Back in the player** under **Settings → Playback →
Player** to open it with **Back** too. It's in every app except on TVs, where Live TV's preview is
the small player.

![Home in the desktop app with Project Hail Mary featured, and the mini player in the bottom corner playing Sintel, with its title, a pause button and a close button below the video](/changelog/v2.38/mini-player.webp)

### Subtitles in the browser and on TVs

In a browser and in the LG and Samsung TV apps, the player now draws ASS and SSA subtitles with
libass, the same renderer mpv uses. Their styling, positions, signs, karaoke, animations and fonts
show as the subtitle's author made them, instead of as plain text, so anime fansubs look as they do
in the desktop and Android apps.

- **Tracks inside the file.** The subtitle tracks inside an MKV or MP4 file now show in the
  subtitle menu, each with its format, as in the apps: ASS, SRT, WebVTT, MP4's own text subtitles,
  and PGS, the picture subtitles on Blu-rays. ASS tracks use the fonts attached to the file. The
  player reads only the subtitles and fonts it needs, not the whole file.
- **Subtitles that line themselves up.** A subtitle from your addons, or a file you add, lines up
  with the file's own subtitles by itself, and one made for another frame rate is retimed first, as
  in the desktop and Android apps.
- **Nothing to set up.** It works by itself, and where libass can't run, styled subtitles show as
  plain text, as before.
- **It follows the player**: the subtitle delay, subtitles moving up above the controls, how the
  video fits the screen, and the mini player.
- **Limits.** In a browser, a file's own tracks, and lining up with them, work only if its host
  lets the browser read it. Streams through AIOStreams' proxy or its usenet streaming do, and so
  does TorBox. The LG and Samsung apps read any host. A font a subtitle names without including
  falls back to a default one, and very heavy typesetting can drop frames on a slow device.

ASS subtitles from your addons now keep their styling in the desktop and Android apps too, where
before they arrived as plain text.

### The WebGPU engine

A browser plays only what it can decode, so MKV files, HEVC video and surround audio often fail.
The new **WebGPU** engine, under **Settings → Playback → Player → Engine**, reads and decodes the
file in the page and draws it with the graphics card. With it, a browser plays HEVC, Dolby Vision,
AC-3, E-AC-3 with Atmos, TrueHD and DTS. It's experimental, so it's off until you choose it.

- **It steps aside by itself.** If it can't play a version, when it opens or partway through, the
  browser's own player takes over at the same point, with the same audio and subtitles.
- **The rest of the player comes with it**: an audio track menu, which a browser's own player
  doesn't have, the subtitles above, the mini player, and a stats panel on **I**.
- **Anime upscaling and shaders.** It upscales anime as the apps do, and takes your own `.wgsl`
  shaders. See [Anime upscaling](#anime-upscaling).
- **Where it shows.** In a browser with WebGPU, such as Chrome, Edge or Firefox, on a page opened
  over HTTPS. The TV apps keep the TV's own player.

### Ratings from each source

Title pages show a badge for each score your metadata addon sends: IMDb, Rotten Tomatoes' critics
and audience, Metacritic, Letterboxd, RogerEbert.com, Trakt, TMDB and MyAnimeList. Where room is
short, as on Home, the first three show. AIOMetadata sends them once its critic ratings are on, and
[custom CSS](/reference/web-app-css) can hide the ones you don't want.

![Severance's page in the desktop app, with its IMDb, Rotten Tomatoes, Metacritic and Trakt scores beside its age rating, above its summary and Resume](/changelog/v2.38/title.webp)

### Title pages

- **Episodes** show their title first, with their air date and a menu button, and a bar slides to
  the season that's open.
- **Unaired episodes** are marked as such. The app asks before playing or downloading one, and
  passes over them when playing on or downloading a season. **Mark unaired episodes** under
  **Settings → Playback** turns it off, for shows whose air dates are often wrong.
- **No spoilers.** **Blur episodes you haven't started**, under **Settings → Interface**, blurs
  their pictures, titles and descriptions until you're on one.
- **Titles without details.** A title no addon in your configuration has details for now opens,
  with a page saying a metadata addon is needed. A film still plays from it.

### Home

![Home in the desktop app with Modern Family featured, Play and More info below its summary, and the Severance card in Continue watching grown into a preview with its episode, air date, time left and synopsis](/changelog/v2.38/card-preview.webp)

- **Play and More info** sit on the featured title, for shows too. **Time on each title** under
  **Settings → Interface** sets how long it stays on each, and a swipe, or a drag with the mouse,
  moves it on sooner.
- **Previews.** While the featured title rotates, a card you rest on with a mouse or a remote grows
  into a bigger preview with its details. **Previews** under **Settings → Interface** turns them
  off.
- **Favourite channels** get a row of their own.

### On phones

![Three phones: Home with Project Hail Mary centred and Play below it, Severance's page with its scores and a labelled row of actions ending in More, and its second season's episodes, each with its title first and its air date](/changelog/v2.38/phones.webp)

- **Centred.** The featured title and a title's heading sit in the middle of the screen, and what's
  over the artwork fades as you scroll.
- **Actions in a row.** Below **Play**, a title's actions have labels: **Trailer**, **Watched**,
  **Favourite** and the ones that fit, with the rest under **More**.
- **Opening a title** grows its card into the page.
- **Back swipe.** In the Android app, Android's back gesture slides the page away under your
  finger.

### Getting around

- **Tabs along the top.** On a computer or a tablet held sideways, the tabs can run along the top
  instead of down the side, under **Settings → Interface**. They, and the bar at the bottom of a
  phone, can show their icons alone.
- **Start on.** **Start on** under **Settings → Interface** picks the page the app opens on.
- **The bottom bar** slides away while you scroll down, and back as you scroll up.
- **Liquid glass** now bends what's behind it like a lens, with a lit rim.

### In the player

- **Paused.** Left paused for a few seconds, the player dims and shows what's playing: the title,
  the episode and its synopsis.
- **Chapters.** In the desktop and Android apps, **Chapters** lists a file's chapters in a sheet,
  each with a picture.
- **With a TV remote**, a press of left or right skips, and holding it scrubs through a row of
  pictures of the video. A remote's channel buttons go to the next or previous chapter.
- **Select** plays or pauses while the controls are hidden. Turn off **Show the controls on pause
  and skip**, under **Settings → Playback**, to keep them hidden then.
- **Subtitles move smoothly** up above the controls when they show, and back down after.
- **The time** shows in the player's top bar.
- **Find versions when a title opens**, under **Settings → Playback**, looks for versions as soon
  as a title's page opens, so **Play** doesn't wait.

### The Android app

- **Styled text subtitles in ExoPlayer.** ExoPlayer now draws SRT and other text subtitles with
  libass too, so they look the same in both engines and slide smoothly.
- **Smoother menus.** On a screen with a high refresh rate, the controls and menus over the video
  move at its fastest rate, while the video itself keeps its own.
- **Opening the app** shows its logo until the app is ready, instead of a blank screen.
- **Fuller reports.** **Report a problem** sends more of the app's log, and after a crash, what the
  app was doing just before.

### Smaller things

- **Playlists** can **Shuffle**, in a mixed order that stays the same until you turn it off, and
  **Repeat**, going back to the first title after the last.
- **Faster on TVs.** Rows scroll with less work, artwork loads nearest first, at the size it's
  drawn, and the player's controls show and hide with about a third of the work they took.
- **Trailers on LG and Samsung TVs** now play, in the TV's own player.
- **Licences.** **About** lists the open source projects in the app, with their licences.
- **More to style with custom CSS.** The new pages, the mini player, the player's top bar and
  chapters, and the episode states have hooks. See [Web app CSS](/reference/web-app-css).

## The apps in your language

The AIOStreams apps now come in **12 languages besides English**: Chinese (Simplified), French,
German, Greek, Indonesian, Italian, Malay, Polish, Portuguese, Portuguese (Brazil), Spanish and
Turkish. Choose yours under **Settings → Interface → Language**, or leave it on **System** to
follow the device.

A language shows in the apps once at least half of it is translated, so a half-done translation
doesn't leave you switching between languages. Dutch, Swedish, Norwegian, Finnish, Slovenian and
Catalan have been started and need help. Translations are made on
[Hosted Weblate](https://hosted.weblate.org/engage/aiostreams/), with no coding needed, and the
[translating guide](/guides/translating) explains the rest.

## The media server

For Jellyfin apps signed in to your configuration:

- **Live TV.** The channels, their groups and the guide show as Jellyfin's Live TV, and past
  programmes with catch-up play from their start.
- **Ratings.** A title's IMDb score is its community rating and its Rotten Tomatoes score its
  critic rating, from your addons' [ratings](#ratings-from-each-source).
- **Titles without details** open, named by their id, with a summary saying no addon has details
  for them, rather than failing.
- **Played at the right point.** The AIOStreams apps send the length of the file they played, so a
  title is marked played at the right point even when its runtime from metadata is minutes off.

## The configure page

- **Who gets this.** The people button beside each addon in **My Addons** and each catalog in
  **Catalogs** chooses who gets it: the base config, each variant you install from a link, and on a
  media server, each user. Tick or untick a row, and the configure page writes the variant lines for
  you. See [who gets an addon or catalog](/reference/config-expressions#who-gets-an-addon-or-catalog).
- **The order of users.** Drag users in the **Users** tab into the order Jellyfin apps' sign-in
  pickers list them in. The primary user stays first.
- **Your own poster service.** **Custom URL** under **Services → Posters** takes a poster address
  of your own, with placeholders such as `{imdb_id}` for each title's id.
- **New in the marketplace**: a **Cinemeta** addon, and **SearchNZB** among the Newznab indexers.
- **Back-to-back selectors** in variants narrow the same list, so
  `presets[type=torrentio][options.name*="4K"]` is only the Torrentio instances with 4K in their
  name.

## For addon developers

- **Live TV.** A catalog of type `tv` or `channel` is a list of channels, with `genre` for their
  groups, and a guide in Stremio's own format fills the programme guide, in Stremio too. Channel
  numbers, logos and catch-up are covered in [Live TV](/reference/addon-protocol/live-tv).
- **Ratings.** `ratings` on a meta lists each source's score, in the order to show them. See
  [Metadata](/reference/addon-protocol/metadata#ratings).
- **An addon SDK.** [`@aiostreams/addon-sdk`](https://github.com/AIOStreams/addon-sdk) handles the
  routing, the configuration in the URL, a configure page built from your settings, and checks of
  every answer, on Node, Bun, Deno and Cloudflare Workers. The new
  [addon development guide](/guides/addon-development) walks through building one, trying it in
  AIOStreams and hosting it.

## For self-hosters

### The Xtream addon

The Xtream addon's settings are under **Settings → Built-ins**: how long a request may take
(`BUILTIN_XTREAM_TIMEOUT`), its user agent (`BUILTIN_XTREAM_USER_AGENT`), how many requests go to
one provider at once (`BUILTIN_XTREAM_HOST_CONCURRENCY`), how often channel lists and guides are
fetched again (`BUILTIN_XTREAM_LINEUP_TTL`, `BUILTIN_XTREAM_GUIDE_TTL`), and how much memory each
may use (`BUILTIN_XTREAM_LINEUP_MEMORY`, `BUILTIN_XTREAM_GUIDE_MEMORY`).

### New settings

- **Allow search engine indexing**, under **Settings → Branding** (`ALLOW_SEARCH_INDEXING`, off).
  See [Upgrading](#upgrading).
- **Max variant work per request**, under **Settings → User Limits** (`MAX_VARIANT_WORK`,
  100000), in place of six variant limits. See [Upgrading](#upgrading).
- **Cinemeta**'s address, timeout and user agent, under **Settings → Presets** (`CINEMETA_URL`,
  `DEFAULT_CINEMETA_TIMEOUT`, `DEFAULT_CINEMETA_USER_AGENT`).

## Upgrading

Nothing in this release changes install URLs or needs a manual migration. A few defaults change.

**Search engines.** Every page an instance serves now asks search engines not to list it, with an
`X-Robots-Tag: noindex` header, so your configure page drops out of search results over the next
weeks. For a public instance you want people to find, turn on **Allow search engine indexing**
under **Settings → Branding** (`ALLOW_SEARCH_INDEXING`).

**Variant limits.** Variants on one request now share a work budget, **Max variant work per
request** (`MAX_VARIANT_WORK`, 100000), where each instruction costs one plus one for every entry
it looks through and every value it writes. It replaces `MAX_VARIANT_SCRIPT_LENGTH`,
`MAX_VARIANT_TOTAL_SCRIPT_CHARACTERS`, `MAX_VARIANT_TOTAL_INSTRUCTIONS`, `MAX_VARIANT_PATH_MATCHES`,
`MAX_VARIANT_VALUE_DEPTH` and `MAX_VARIANT_PATH_SEGMENTS`, which no longer do anything; scripts have
no length limit of their own beyond `MAX_JSON_BODY_SIZE`. **Max variants** (`MAX_VARIANTS`) now
defaults to 50, up from 10.

**Resume points.** **Minimum resume percentage** under **Settings → Watch State**
(`WATCH_STATE_MIN_RESUME_PERCENT`) now defaults to 0, so every position you stop at is kept. Set it
back to 5 to leave titles stopped in their first moments out of Continue Watching.

**Unaired episodes in Jellyfin apps.** **Mark unaired episodes**, in the media server's settings
under **Save & Install**, is now off unless you turned it on. Jellyfin apps list upcoming episodes
as normal ones, since some hide unaired episodes altogether. The AIOStreams app still marks them and
asks before playing one.
