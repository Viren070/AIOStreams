**Watch together** with friends and family. A watch party keeps everyone's player at the same
moment, with **chat and reactions** over the video, whether you are on the same server or on
different ones. Each person plays from their own server and addons, and the relay between you
never learns what you watch.

The desktop and Android apps show **previews on the seek bar** as you point, drag or skip, and
**subtitles line themselves up**: subtitles from your addons, in any language, match the
subtitles inside the video a moment after you turn them on, retimed first if they were made for
another frame rate.

Any app can **play a title on another device**, or take over whatever another device is already
playing, and become its remote, whether that device has an AIOStreams app or a Jellyfin app open.

In every app, **quick filters** and **badges** make a long list of versions easy to scan,
**people** turn up in search, **playlists** play on from one title to the next, **Random pick**
chooses from your favourites or a show's episodes, and **pressing play** opens on the title's
logo and fades into the video. The desktop and Android apps gain **sound settings** such as
dialogue boost and night mode, the desktop app plays **HDR and Dolby Vision on a Mac**, and when
something goes wrong, **Report a problem** sends the app's log with a code to quote.

The apps can now be **translated**: German and Polish are already done, and Italian is under way.
If you speak another language, [you can help](#help-translate-the-apps).

For self-hosters, an instance can relay parties for friends on other servers, keep the reports
its users send, and style the app for everyone with CSS. This release also brings
[security fixes](#upgrading), among them one for a remote code execution vulnerability in
Node.js, so update soon.

## Watch together

Press **Watch together** on a title's page, in the player, or in the app's navigation, and pick
where the party runs. When anyone pauses, seeks or skips, it happens for everyone, and the others
see who did it. Each person plays the title from their own server with their own addons, so
nobody shares a stream, a debrid account or a login.

- **Every app with its own player.** Parties work in a browser, the desktop app, the Android app
  on phones, tablets and TVs, and the LG and Samsung TV apps. A player in another app, such as
  VLC or Infuse, can't follow a party.
- **On your server or across servers.** A party can run on the server you are signed in to, or
  on a relay, for people on other servers. A relay is any AIOStreams server that lets parties in.
  The apps suggest `aiostreams.viren070.me` unless your server's owner picked another.
- **Inviting.** Send the link, read out the ten-character code, which is easy to type with a
  remote, or show the QR code on a TV for a phone to scan. People on the same server see its
  parties under **Join a party** without an invite. Before joining, the app shows whose party it
  is, who is in it and what it is watching, with everyone's avatar.
- **Chat and reactions.** Messages show over the video for a few seconds and stay in the party's
  **Chat**, and reactions float up the side. On a TV, which has no keyboard, the reactions take
  the message box's place. Either can stay off the video under **Settings → Playback → Watch
  parties**, and **Reactions** there picks the ones you can send.
- **The same release.** Each person's app picks the version closest to the one the party started
  with, the same file where they have it.
- **Playing on.** A skip button skips the intro or credits for everyone, and the next episode
  plays for everyone together. The host can let only themselves control playback, close the party
  to new people, and remove someone.
- **Stalls and dropouts.** When someone's video stalls for more than 3 seconds, everyone waits
  for them, for up to 10 seconds. When your connection drops, your player carries on, and once
  it's back, it catches up without pausing anyone.

Progress and watch history stay your own: each person's server records what they watched, as it
would without a party. A party holds up to 16 people.

<Callout type="info">
  A relay never learns what you watch. Names, avatars, chat, reactions and what's
  playing are encrypted on your device with a key from the invite, which the
  relay never receives. It sees only when the party plays, pauses and seeks.
</Callout>

[Watch together](/apps/watch-together) has the details, and Jellyfin apps get their own way to
watch together through [SyncPlay](#the-media-server).

## New in the app

Everything here works in every app, in a browser too, unless it says otherwise.

### Previews on the seek bar

In the desktop and Android apps, a picture of the video shows above the seek bar wherever you
point, drag or skip to, with the mouse, a finger, the keyboard or a TV remote.

The apps make the pictures themselves, from the keyframes in the file you are playing, so they
work with any addon and any debrid or usenet stream, with nothing to set up. They read only a
little of the file for each picture, in the background, without holding up playback, on TVs too.
HDR and Dolby Vision videos, profile 5 included, show their real colours.

Previews work for MKV and MP4 files. Each picture reads a little of the file, so **Seek bar
previews** under **Settings → Playback → Controls** turns them off to save data.

### Subtitles that line themselves up

In the desktop and Android apps, subtitles from your subtitle addons, or a file you add, line up
with the subtitles inside the video by themselves, a moment after you turn them on. They match
in any language, and subtitles made for a version at another frame rate, such as a 25 fps
release, are retimed to fit first.

![Tears of Steel in the desktop app with a Spanish subtitle, made for a 25 fps release, in time with the actor, and the subtitle menu's Timing page reading −2.1s, Retimed and synced automatically](/changelog/v2.37/subtitle-sync.webp)

For MKV and MP4 files, the apps read when the video's own subtitles show from the file's index,
so the whole film lines up straight away. Blu-ray picture subtitles count too, so remuxes that
carry only those work as well. A video with no subtitles of its own has nothing to line up with, and its subtitles play as
they come.

When a subtitle is still out, **Timing** in the subtitle menu moves it, says how its timing was
set, and remembers it for that subtitle of that version. **Sync automatically** hands a subtitle
you moved yourself back to the apps. **Style** in the subtitle menu, and **Sound** in the audio
menu, put their settings in the player too.

### Play on another device

**Play on another device**, on a title's page or in its menu, sends the title to another device
and turns the app in your hand into its remote: pause, seek, skip, stop, and pick the audio and
subtitles. While it plays, a button at the bottom of the screen brings the remote back.

![The desktop app playing Tears of Steel as Living Room, with a Spanish subtitle, beside a phone showing the remote for Living Room: the title, its position, the playback controls, Stop and the Spanish subtitle picked](/changelog/v2.37/remote.webp)

- **Which devices.** Any device signed in to your configuration with an AIOStreams app open, and
  Jellyfin apps that can be remote controlled. A device only shows up while its app is open, and
  there is no Chromecast or AirPlay.
- **From Jellyfin apps too.** The **Play on** or cast menu in a Jellyfin app lists the AIOStreams
  apps, and plays on and controls them the same way.
- **Anything that's playing.** You don't have to send a title first: **Activity**, and **Other
  devices** in the remote, show what each device is playing, and choosing one opens its remote,
  however it started playing.
- **Who controls what.** Each user controls the devices signed in as themselves, and the primary
  user controls everyone's.
- **Links.** The desktop and Android apps open `aiostreams://` links to a title's page or to play
  it, so a page elsewhere can open a title in them.

### Quick filters and badges for versions

A title's versions can now be searched with words or with a regular expression between slashes,
such as `/2160p|4k/`. Save a search as a **quick filter** and it shows as a pill above the list,
with how many versions it finds. On a TV, where there is no search box, the pills are how to
narrow a long list.

**Badges** label the versions a search matches, as text in your own colours or as an image, so
a remux or a Dolby Vision release stands out at a glance.

![Dune: Part Two's versions in the desktop app with the Atmos quick filter picked, leaving four of its seven versions, each with its resolution, source, codec, size and bitrate, and Dolby Vision and Dolby Atmos logo badges](/changelog/v2.37/versions.webp)

_Dolby, Dolby Vision and Dolby Atmos are trademarks of Dolby Laboratories._

- **Set them up** under **Settings → Playback → Versions**. They are saved to your account, so
  they follow you to every device.
- **They read more than the name.** Searches match what your formatter writes as well as the
  version's details: its file and folder names, resolution, quality, audio and visual tags,
  languages, release group, editions, SeaDex and the stream expressions it matched. A search for
  `atmos` finds Atmos versions even where your formatter shows Atmos as an icon.
- **Share them.** **Copy as JSON** copies your quick filters or badges, and **Import** takes
  pasted JSON or a file's address. A badge pack's address stays linked, so its changes come
  through. Badge packs made for Fusion and Nuvio work too.
- A title with hundreds of versions opens quickly, since the list draws a page at a time.

[Version filters and badges](/reference/version-badges) has the format.

### Sound

The desktop and Android apps have new sound settings, under **Settings → Audio** and in the
player's audio menu:

- **Dialogue boost** raises the centre channel, where surround sound puts the dialogue.
- **Night mode** evens out loud and quiet scenes, for listening at a low volume.
- **Delay** moves the sound earlier or later, for wireless headphones that lag behind the
  picture.
- **Formats** picks which of Dolby Digital, Dolby Digital Plus, DTS, DTS-HD and Dolby TrueHD pass
  through to your TV or receiver as they are. The app decodes the rest.
- **Turn other surround sound into Dolby Digital** is for a receiver on an optical or ARC link,
  which otherwise gets those formats as stereo.
- The desktop app picks the output **Device**, and the Android app now mixes surround sound down
  to the **Channels** your speakers take, keeping the dialogue in.

In every app, **Audio types** under **Settings → Audio** puts the main audio, audio description
and commentary in your order, so an audio description track doesn't play unless you want it. The
server picks Jellyfin apps' default track from the same order.

### HDR and Dolby Vision on a Mac

The desktop app now plays HDR on a Mac. On a screen that shows HDR, such as a MacBook Pro's,
HDR10, HLG and Dolby Vision videos play in HDR rather than tone mapped down to standard range,
and fit the screen's brightness as you change it.

On macOS 14 or later, **Video output** under **Settings → Desktop app** picks what draws the
video, from the next start:

- **mpv**, the default, draws it itself, with your `mpv.conf` and shaders, as on Windows.
- **System** has macOS draw it, which uses less power and shows Dolby Vision as the screen
  supports it. Shaders and mpv's own picture options don't apply.
- **Compatibility** is the older way mpv draws, for a Mac where the others fail. Older versions
  of macOS always use it.

HDR on a Mac builds on work by [@adityabakare](https://github.com/adityabakare).

### Report a problem

In the desktop and Android apps, **Report a problem** under **Settings → Desktop app** or
**Settings → Android app** sends the app's versions and recent log to your server, with a code to
give whoever looks into it. Choose to send it to the developer too, and your server passes it on
with passwords, tokens and configuration IDs taken out. After a crash, the Android app offers to
send what Android recorded about it the next time it opens.

### Switching users

- **Select your avatar** to switch users. **Sign out**, **Change server** and the configure page
  have moved to **Settings → Account**.
- **A user with a PIN stays unlocked** on that device, even after the app restarts, until they
  switch away or sign out. Switch away before handing the device to someone else.
- **Your subtitle look** is saved to your account, so it follows you to other devices, and each
  user keeps their own.

### The Android app

- **Steadier on a slow connection.** The player now holds part of the video in memory: ahead, to
  ride out a slow or unsteady connection, and behind, so seeking back is instant. **Cache size**
  under **Settings → Playback → Player** sets how much. **Automatic** sizes it from the device's
  memory, and on mobile data the app reads only a little ahead.
- **Skips that don't wait.** Intros and other segments set to skip aren't downloaded at all, and
  for a segment with a skip button, the start of what follows is fetched early.
- **Faster seeking** in ExoPlayer, which now lands on a nearby keyframe.
- **Faster 10-bit HEVC in mpv**, when it decodes in software on a device whose hardware can't.
- **Less memory** for styled subtitles.
- **On Android TV**, the keyboard floats over the app as a compact panel instead of covering the
  screen.
- **On the TV's home screen.** On Android TV and Google TV, the home screen's **Continue
  watching** or **Play Next** row lists what you're partway through and your shows' next episodes,
  and selecting one plays it in the app.
- **A second Back to leave.** Back on Home asks for another press before the app closes, so one
  press too many doesn't close it.

### Smaller things

- **Subtitle fonts and shadows.** **Font** under **Settings → Subtitles** offers sans serif,
  serif, monospace, typewriter, casual and cursive, **Shadow** under **Outline and shadow** adds a
  drop shadow, and **Height** can now put subtitles lower than usual, down to the screen's edge.
- **The seek bar shows everything loaded**, each stretch of the video the player holds, behind you
  as well as ahead.
- **More versions play.** Versions that need request headers, and YouTube videos offered as
  versions, now play in the desktop and Android apps, and the ones with headers download too.
- **Animated posters.** A title whose metadata has an animated poster plays it while you hover
  over or move to its card.
- **Trailers** play in your audio and subtitle languages.
- **Rows with a remote or keyboard.** Moving along a row keeps the card you're on where the row
  starts, as TV apps do, and coming back to a row returns to the card you left. Tabs, such as a
  show's seasons, switch as you move onto them.
- **Time left and when it ends.** The player's time sits above the seek bar, beside the time the
  title ends at the speed it's playing. Select the time to show what's left instead.
- **Continue watching** puts what you watched most recently first, whether it's a title you're
  partway through or a show's next episode.
- **Variants can tell the apps apart.** The apps say which app, device and platform they are,
  the network on Android, and whether they are finding a version to download, in headers a
  variant's condition reads with `header()`. A variant can, say, list smaller versions for the
  Android app on mobile data. See
  [the apps' own headers](/reference/config-expressions#the-apps-own-headers).
- **Cache size in the desktop app.** **Cache size** under **Settings → Playback → Player** now sets
  how much the desktop app's player holds in memory too. **Automatic** sizes it from the
  computer's memory, and a `demuxer-max-bytes` line in your `mpv.conf` takes its place.
- **Player tooltips.** The player's buttons say what they do when you point at them or move to
  them with a remote.
- **The calendar's agenda** opens at today.
- **More to style with custom CSS.** Poster cards say whether a title is a favourite and whether
  it's a movie or a show, and party members, their messages and the player's cover can be styled
  too. See [Web app CSS](/reference/web-app-css).

## Help translate the apps

The AIOStreams apps can now be translated. Every page, menu, setting and player control is ready
for other languages, and the apps lay themselves out right to left for languages such as Arabic
and Hebrew. German and Polish were translated in full before this release was out, thanks to
Florian Otto and hamburgercowgoldfish for German and Krzysztof for Polish, and Nicola Bortoletto
has started on Italian. Every other language is waiting for someone to start it, and that's where
you come in.

Translations are made on [Hosted Weblate](https://hosted.weblate.org/engage/aiostreams/), which
is open source and needs no coding: sign in, pick your language or start a new one, and
translate one line at a time. What you translate ships with the next nightly and release, in the
browser and in every app. The [translating guide](/guides/translating) explains placeholders and
plurals.

Once your language is there, choose it under **Settings → Interface → Language**. **Automatic**
follows the device, and each user keeps their own choice.

## The media server

For Jellyfin apps signed in to your configuration:

- **SyncPlay.** Jellyfin apps with SyncPlay, such as the Jellyfin web client, Jellyfin Media
  Player and Streamyfin, can watch together. Groups are shared between the apps signed in to the
  same configuration, or the whole server if its owner allows it. They are separate from the
  AIOStreams app's parties.
- **Remote control.** Jellyfin apps that take remote control show up under
  [Play on another device](#play-on-another-device), and a Jellyfin app's **Play on** or cast
  menu can play on and control the AIOStreams apps.
- **People.** Searching finds people, and person pages and More Like This can come from your
  addons, [as in the app](#people).
- **Up to 500 libraries.** Every catalog can now be a library: the limit went from 20 to 500. A
  configuration's catalog requests to its addons queue up to 8 at a time, so an app that loads
  every library's latest row at once doesn't flood your addons.
- **Playlists.** Each user's [playlists](#playlists) are kept on the server, so Jellyfin apps
  share them with the AIOStreams app, and once a user has one, a **Playlists** library shows it.
- **Default audio track.** The server picks the default audio track by your
  [audio types](#sound) as well as your languages.

## The configure page

**Addons → My Addons** has a tidier layout. The search box and the category filters, which now
show how many addons each holds, stay at the top as you scroll a long list. Tick addons, or every
one shown, and a single **Actions** menu turns them on or off, edits the fields they share, sets
their category or deletes them.

Adding an addon that isn't in the Marketplace is also easier to find. It has always worked
through the **Custom** addon, and now **Add by URL** sits at the bottom of My Addons, showing the
addon's name and description from its manifest before you add it.

## For addon developers

- **Person metas.** Give a person in a meta's `people` an `id`, and AIOStreams opens it as a
  `person` meta from whichever addon serves that id, with `birthday`, `deathday`, `birthPlace`
  and `credits`. A `tmdb:` id is worth giving even without person metas, since the TMDB page is
  then that person's and not whoever is best known by the same name. AIOMetadata's `tmdbId` and
  `tvdbId` on cast are read too. See [People](/reference/addon-protocol/people).
- **People search.** A `person` catalog with a required `search` extra answers people searches in
  the apps and in Jellyfin apps.
- **Similar titles.** A catalog that declares a required `similar` extra lists titles like the
  one it's given, which the apps show as **More Like This**. See
  [Similar Titles](/reference/addon-protocol/similar).
- **Animated posters.** `animatedPoster` on a meta is an animated image the apps play while its
  card is hovered or focused. See [Metadata](/reference/addon-protocol/metadata).
- **Country codes.** A meta's `countries` can hold codes as well as names, and every spelling of
  a country shows by the same name.

## For self-hosters

### Hosting watch parties

Parties for the people signed in to your instance are on by default. Whether others can use it
as a relay is up to you, under **Settings → Watch Parties**:

- **Relay** (`PARTY_RELAY`, `off`). `off` lets only your own users start and join parties.
  `users` lets your users start parties that anyone with an invite can join, from any server,
  and needs `AIOSTREAMS_AUTH_REQUIRED`. `public` lets anyone start and join parties, for a relay
  meant for everyone.
- **Who sees parties** (`PARTY_SCOPE`, `configuration`). Who sees each other's parties without an
  invite: the users of one configuration, or with `instance`, everyone on the server.
- **Default relay** (`PARTY_DEFAULT_RELAY`, `https://aiostreams.viren070.me`). The relay the apps
  suggest for parties with people on other servers while your own relay is off. Leave it empty
  to suggest none.
- **Max parties** (`PARTY_MAX_PARTIES`, 1000).

To let friends in through your instance, a reverse proxy only needs to open `/party/`, with
WebSocket upgrades, and `/assets/` to the internet. Guests play from their own servers and only
timing passes through yours, never video.

Parties live in memory, or in Redis when `REDIS_URI` is set. A restart doesn't end them, and with
Redis, replicas behind one address share parties and SyncPlay groups, so members on different
replicas watch together. [For instance owners](/apps/watch-together#for-instance-owners) has the
details.

### App reports

**Dashboard → Reports** lists the reports your users send from the apps, by the code they were
given, with the app's versions, device and recent log. Under **Settings → App Reports**:

- **App reports** (`APP_REPORTS`, `users`). `off` keeps none, and the apps offer no way to send
  one. `users` keeps your own users' reports. `public` also keeps reports other servers pass on,
  for a developer's server.
- **Developer for app reports** (`APP_REPORTS_FORWARD_TO`, `https://aiostreams.viren070.me`).
  Where a report goes when its sender also sends it to the developer. Leave it empty to offer no
  such choice.
- **Max app reports** (`APP_REPORTS_MAX`, 1000) and **App report retention**
  (`APP_REPORTS_RETENTION`, 7 days).

Each address can send 10 reports an hour, set under **Settings → Rate Limits**
(`APP_REPORTS_RATE_LIMIT_WINDOW`, `APP_REPORTS_RATE_LIMIT_MAX_REQUESTS`).

### Styling the app for everyone

**App custom CSS** under **Settings → Branding** (`APP_CUSTOM_CSS`) styles the AIOStreams apps for
everyone signed in to your instance. Each person's own custom CSS applies on top of it, and
anyone can turn it off with **Use this server's CSS** under **Settings → Theme** in the app. See
[Web app CSS](/reference/web-app-css).

### New settings

- **Watch parties**, **app reports** and **App custom CSS**, as above.
- **Max libraries per configuration**, under **Settings → Media server**
  (`JELLYFIN_MAX_LIBRARIES`), now defaults to 500, which is also the most.
- **Concurrent catalog requests**, under **Settings → Media server**
  (`JELLYFIN_CATALOG_CONCURRENCY`, 8): how many catalog requests one configuration's media server
  sends to its addons at once.

## Upgrading

Nothing in this release changes install URLs or needs a manual migration. A few things are worth
checking.

**Security fixes.** This is the first release since the Docker image moved to Debian 13, so it
comes with the latest Node.js 24, which fixes a remote code execution vulnerability. The last
release's image is on Debian 12, which no longer gets Node.js updates. If you run AIOStreams
without Docker, update Node.js yourself. Many dependencies are updated too, fixing known
vulnerabilities in them. Update soon.

**More libraries.** With **Max libraries per configuration** (`JELLYFIN_MAX_LIBRARIES`) now at
500, configurations with more than 20 catalogs show all of them as libraries. Jellyfin apps that
load every library's latest row when they open now make more catalog requests, up to 8 at a time
per configuration. Set it back to 20 to keep the old limit.

**Reports go to your instance.** The apps' **Report a problem** sends reports to your instance,
which keeps them for 7 days. A report reaches the developer's server only when its sender chooses
that, with passwords, tokens and configuration IDs taken out. Set **App reports**
(`APP_REPORTS`) to `off` to turn reports off, or empty **Developer for app reports**
(`APP_REPORTS_FORWARD_TO`) to keep them on your instance only.

**Parties and the default relay.** Your users can start parties on your instance straight away.
For parties with people on other servers, the apps suggest `aiostreams.viren070.me`, which sees
only when a party plays, pauses and seeks. Empty **Default relay** (`PARTY_DEFAULT_RELAY`) to
suggest none.

**What is on by default.** Watch parties for your own users, reports from your own users, and the
relay suggestion. [New settings](#new-settings) says where to switch each one off. Letting others
use your instance as a relay is off.
