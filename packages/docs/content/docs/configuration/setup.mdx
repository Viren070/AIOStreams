This guide walks you through a complete AIOStreams setup, from choosing where you'll watch to a configuration ready to use in Stremio and the AIOStreams app. It uses a community template that does most of the configuration for you, so you mostly pick options and fill in your keys.

<Callout type="info">
  **Template Credits**

This guide uses a template built and maintained by
[Tam-Taro](https://github.com/Tam-Taro/SEL-Filtering-and-Sorting)
([@tam.taro](https://discord.com) on Discord), who has spent countless hours
perfecting their Stream Expression Language filtering and sorting. It also
incorporates [Vidhin's Regex Patterns](https://github.com/Vidhin05/Releases-Regex),
which bring the Trash Guides release group rankings into AIOStreams.

If you find this useful, consider supporting them:

- Tam-Taro: [ko-fi.com/tamtaro](https://ko-fi.com/tamtaro)
- Vidhin: [ko-fi.com/vidhin](https://ko-fi.com/vidhin)

</Callout>

One configuration serves all of these at once, so you can start with one and add another later.

## Step 2: Get a debrid or Usenet service

A **debrid service** is a paid service that maintains a large cache of torrents on its own servers. When a torrent is first requested, the debrid service downloads it once and keeps it cached. Later requests for the same torrent are served instantly from the cache at high speeds over HTTPS, with no P2P involved on your end.

Streams from debrid-enabled addons are labelled as either **cached** (available instantly) or **uncached** (needs to be downloaded to the debrid service first before you can stream it). For popular content, the vast majority of results will already be cached.

**In Stremio, a service is optional but recommended. The AIOStreams app needs one**, since it doesn't play P2P torrents. P2P streams work without one, provided there are enough seeders, but in many countries (including Germany and the United States) torrenting without a VPN exposes you to ISP warnings, throttling, or fines. A debrid service avoids this, since you stream over HTTPS from the debrid provider's servers. Using one also gives you:

- Instant streaming of cached content at high speeds (4K, HDR, Dolby Atmos without buffering)
- No reliance on seeders
- No VPN required

**Usenet** works too, through Easynews, TorBox or your own Usenet provider: see the [Usenet guide](/guides/usenet).

<Callout type="warn">
  If you choose to use P2P streams **without** a debrid service and without a
  VPN, be aware that your ISP can see your torrent traffic. In some countries
  this can result in warning letters, throttling, or fines. Check the rules in
  your country before proceeding.
</Callout>

## Step 3: Get a TMDB API key

The template uses a TMDB key for title and season/episode matching, which filters out streams for the wrong title or episode. A TVDB key is optional. Some instances provide a TMDB key of their own: the template then says so, and you can leave the field blank.

### TMDB (The Movie Database)

<Steps>
  <Step>
    Go to [themoviedb.org](https://www.themoviedb.org/) and create a free
    account (or sign in).
  </Step>
  <Step>
    Open **Account Settings → API** and click **Create** to request a key.
    Choose **Developer** when asked for the application type.

    <Callout type="tip">
      None of the information on the form needs to be accurate. You can use a
      temporary email address, a fake name, and a made-up phone number. None
      of it is verified.
    </Callout>

  </Step>
  <Step>
    Copy your **API Key** from the API settings page: the 32-character key, not
    the Read Access Token.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### TVDB (The TV Database, optional)

<Steps>
  <Step>
    Go to [thetvdb.com](https://thetvdb.com/) and create a free account (or
    sign in).
  </Step>
  <Step>
    Navigate to [API Information](https://thetvdb.com/api-information) and
    generate a new API key.
  </Step>
  <Step>Copy the API key.</Step>
</Steps>

## Step 4: Open the template

Select a public instance below and click **Open Template**. This takes you directly to that instance's configuration page with the template pre-loaded.

<Callout type="info">
  Not sure which instance to pick? See
  [Public Instances](/getting-started/public-instances) for a comparison.
  **Yeb's** is the recommended starting point for most users. **ElfHosted** is
  a reliable and reputable option but the public instance disables P2P, HTTP,
  and Live stream types. If you need those, consider a
  [private ElfHosted instance](/getting-started/deployment#elfhosted-paid-managed)
  (paid, no restrictions, relaxed rate limits, direct ElfHosted support, and
  33% of your subscription goes to the developer).
</Callout>

<TemplateOpener templateUrl="https://git.tamtaro.de/complete.json" defaultChannel="nightly">
  <Callout type="warn">
    This template requires the following environment variable to be set on your
    instance so that its SEL sync and regex URLs are whitelisted:

    ```sh
    TEMPLATE_URLS=["https://git.tamtaro.de/complete.json"]
    ```

    Optionally, if your instance is access-controlled (login required via
    `AIOSTREAMS_AUTH` / `AIOSTREAMS_AUTH_REQUIRED`) and you want to avoid
    per-user URL restrictions, you can also set the **SEL sync access** and
    **Regex filter access** to `all`. These (and `TEMPLATE_URLS`) are
    runtime settings configurable from the dashboard Settings page, or via the
    matching environment variables:

    ```sh
    SEL_SYNC_ACCESS=all
    REGEX_FILTER_ACCESS=all
    ```

    See [Environment Variables](/configuration/environment-variables) for details

  </Callout>
</TemplateOpener>

## Step 5: Import the template

After clicking **Open Template**, the configure page opens with a short setup that follows the steps on its left.

<Steps>
  <Step>
    **Confirm Import**: because the template comes from a link, AIOStreams
    asks first. Check it is **Tamtaro Complete SEL Setup** by Tamtaro, then
    click **Use This Template Now**.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    **Services**: tick the services you have, under **Debrid** and **Usenet**,
    then click **Continue**. Without a service, click **I don't use any of
    these** and the template sets itself up for P2P.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    **Options**: every option has a description, and the defaults suit most
    people. A few worth a look:

    - **Required Languages**: the languages you watch in. Streams in other
      languages are left out, apart from ones tagged Original, Dual Audio,
      Multi, Dubbed or Unknown, unless you also turn on **Strict Language
      Filtering**.
    - **Formatter Style**: how streams are named. Pick the style made for your
      app if there is one, such as AppleTV or Chillio.
    - **TorBox Tier** _(TorBox only, required)_: your plan, which sets the size
      limit for uncached downloads.
    - **Setup Variants**: **Include Variants** adds extra install links on the
      Save & Install page, such as Debrid Only and Usenet Only.

    **Show 10 advanced options** has the finer controls. Click **Continue**.

  </Step>
  <Step>
    **Credentials**: the API key for each service you picked and your TMDB key
    from step 3. TVDB is optional. Click **Continue**.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    **Review**: the addons, services and credentials the setup will apply.
    Click **Apply setup**. Nothing is saved until the next step.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="info">
  Want to understand what every option does after import? See
  [Configure Options](/configuration/options).
</Callout>

## Step 6: Save and install

After the setup is applied you land on the **Save & Install** page.

<Steps>
  <Step>
    Under **Create Configuration**, enter a password twice and click
    **Create**. Your UUID appears under **Save Configuration**. **Save both
    your UUID and password somewhere safe: there is no way to recover them if
    lost.**

    <Callout type="warn">
      If you see an error like **"Failed to fetch manifest for &lt;addon&gt;"**,
      go to the **Addons** menu and disable the addon mentioned in the error,
      then try clicking **Create** again. This is usually a temporary issue
      with that addon: you can re-enable it and save again later once it
      recovers.

      If you see **"Failed to validate TMDB API Key"**, double-check your TMDB
      API key is correct. You can review it in the **Services** menu under the
      **Metadata** section.
    </Callout>

  </Step>
  <Step>
    **Install to Stremio?** opens next. Sign in with your Stremio account (an
    email and password, or an auth key) and AIOStreams installs itself there
    and updates itself whenever you save. It's optional: **Not now** closes it.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    **Installation Options** has everything else:

    - **Stremio**: **Install to Stremio** opens the Stremio app on this device,
      **Install to Stremio Web** installs through Stremio Web (only if you are
      signed in there, or it installs to a guest account), and **Direct
      Manifest URL** is the link for installing by hand, in Stremio or any app
      that supports Stremio addons.
    - **AIOStreams app**: **Open in browser** opens the app right away, and the
      buttons beside it get the app for your computer, Android device or TV.
      See the [AIOStreams app guide](/apps).
    - **Other apps**: Seanime, Jellyfin apps, Chillio and Aniyomi, each with
      its own instructions.

  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="info">
  Click **Export** in the **Backups** section to download a JSON copy of your
  configuration. You can use this to restore or transfer your setup later.
</Callout>

## Step 7: Add catalogs and metadata

The template brings streams but no catalogs. **[AIOMetadata](https://github.com/cedya77/aiometadata)** adds them, along with each title's details. Configure it first: [Tamtaro's AIOMetadata guide](https://github.com/Tam-Taro/SEL-Filtering-and-Sorting/tree/main?tab=readme-ov-file#%EF%B8%8F-whats-included-for-aiometadata) has ready-made configurations to start from. Where it goes depends on where you watch.

<Tabs items={['Stremio', 'AIOStreams app']}>
  <Tab value="Stremio">
    Install AIOMetadata in Stremio on its own, next to AIOStreams, as Tamtaro's guide describes.

    Stremio then asks AIOMetadata directly, so your changes there show up straight away. Inside AIOStreams, its catalogs would pass through AIOStreams' cache, which on a public instance the instance's owner controls, not you.

  </Tab>
  <Tab value="AIOStreams app">
    The app only shows what your configuration provides, so AIOMetadata goes inside AIOStreams:

    <Steps>
      <Step>
        Copy AIOMetadata's manifest URL. In AIOStreams, go to **Addons →
        Installed**, click **Add by URL** and paste it.
      </Step>
      <Step>
        Move AIOMetadata to the top of **My Addons** with the reorder button, so
        its metadata is used first.
      </Step>
      <Step>Go to **Save & Install** and click **Save**.</Step>
    </Steps>

    After you change AIOMetadata's catalogs, press the refresh button on the **Catalogs** card (**Addons → Installed → Catalogs**) and save, so AIOStreams picks up the new list.

    Using Stremio too? Keep AIOMetadata out of your Stremio install with a variant, so its catalogs don't show twice there: see [Different addons for the apps and Stremio](/apps/media-server#different-addons-for-the-apps-and-stremio).

  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Making changes

To edit your configuration after installation:

- In **Stremio**, click **Configure** next to the AIOStreams addon.
- Or go to `https://your-instance/stremio/configure`, click **Sign In** and enter your UUID and password.

Once you've made your changes, go to **Save & Install** and click **Save**.

<Callout type="info">
  Most changes apply immediately without reinstalling. Only reinstall if a
  pop-up appears telling you to.
</Callout>
